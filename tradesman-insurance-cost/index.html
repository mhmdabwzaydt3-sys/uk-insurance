Who Needs It? Why Is Tradesman Insurance Required?

You need tradesman insurance if you work as a tradesperson charging for work at someone else's property or site. Not all insurers cover all trades, but most UK sites, domestic clients and contractors will ask for proof. Cost varies by risk.

Checklist – You Likely Need Cover If You:
  • Sole trader builder, carpenter, roofer, bricklayer, painter/decorator or multi-trade handyman working in customer homes or sites
  • Employ one or more staff, labourers or apprentices – then employers' liability at least £5m is legally compulsory under Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 in Great Britain – VERIFIED
  • Use scaffolding, ladders, roofing work or work at height above 10-15m (height limits vary – check wording)
  • Handle heat work (blowtorch, bitumen boiler, welding) – must be declared, hot work exclusions are common
  • Carry £2,000+ of hand tools, power tools or materials in a van overnight – theft risk high for tools
  • Work on commercial sites where principal contractor requires £2m or £5m public liability and proof of insurance
  • Provide design, advice or certification – you may need professional indemnity in addition to public liability
  • Sub-contract to others – you may need contingent liability and need to check labour-only vs bona-fide sub-contractor status

What Does Tradesman Insurance Cover?

Tradesman insurance covers your legal liability for accidental injury to the public or damage to their property arising from your trade. Then it layers optional covers you choose. You pay for limits you select. Check your policy wording, as not all insurers cover working at height or heat.

What's Included – Typical Limits

Core Liability
  • Public Liability: £1m, £2m or £5m – VARIABLE by contract. £2m most common for domestic trades – HYPOTHETICAL typical, not legal minimum.
  • Employers' Liability: £10m typical market limit, legal minimum £5m under 1969 Act – VERIFIED
  • Products Liability: Usually included with PL up to same limit for materials you supply
Trade Property & Injury
  • Tools Cover: £1,000 – £10,000 hand / power tools, often with single item limit £750-£1,500 – VARIABLE
  • Own Plant / Hired Plant: Optional, specified sums, excess applies
  • Personal Accident: Weekly benefit e.g. £150-£350 for temporary total disablement – illustrative, check wording
Contract & Legal
  • Contract Works / Own Works: Optional, covers work in progress on site against fire, theft, collapse
  • Professional Indemnity: Optional, e.g. £50k-£250k for faulty design/advice
  • Legal Expenses: Optional £50k-£100k for HMRC, employment, contract disputes

Limits shown are VARIABLE market norms, not guaranteed. Your quote determines actual limits. Verify wording on insurer PDF before buying.

Optional Coverages You Can Add

  • Contract works / All Risks: Covers materials and work-in-progress if a partially built wall collapses or is damaged by storm. Critical for builders and roofers.
  • Hired-in plant insurance: If you hire a digger for £2,500/week, you are liable for its loss. This add-on covers that.
  • Tools in vehicle overnight: Many standard tools covers exclude overnight van storage unless van is alarmed and tools hidden. Ask for extension.
  • Public liability height increase: Standard may cap at 10m. Roofers often need 15-20m+ extension – cost increases.
  • Heat work extension: For bitumen, torch-on felt, plumbing solder. Requires risk assessment log.

What is NOT Covered / Strictly Excluded / Common Mistakes

This is where tradespeople lose money. You think public liability covers poor workmanship. It does not. You need to know exclusions before you buy, or your claim is rejected.

Danger Box – Strict Exclusions (Check Wording)
  • Faulty workmanship / rectification: If your wall is uneven and must be rebuilt, PL does not pay to redo it. It may only cover resultant damage to third party property, not your own work.
  • Gradual damage & wear and tear: Slow leak from incorrect pipe fitting over 6 months often excluded as gradual, not accidental sudden damage.
  • Working at height above insured limit / undeclared heat: If you declared 10m max but work at 15m on a scaffold in Bristol, height exclusion may void claim. Similarly torch-on work without hot-work permit.
  • Tools left in unattended van without forcible entry / own property: Insurers require evidence of break-in, van alarm, hidden storage. Tools stored at home may need separate specification.
  • Asbestos, pollution, and defective products you knew were faulty: Asbestos disturbance or pollution typically excluded under general tradesman PL.
  • Subcontractor work when labour-only undeclared: If you use labour-only subcontractors but insured as sole trader with no employers' liability, injury to them may not be covered – legal breach under 1969 Act.

Exclusions are VARIABLE by insurer – verify in your AXA, Aviva, Hiscox policy wording PDF – don't rely on summary page.

How Does Tradesman Insurance Work? Step-by-Step

Success Box – How It Works in the Right Order
  1. Assess trade and turnover: List exact trades (e.g., carpentry, roofing <10m, no flat roofing with torch), annual turnover (e.g., £60k), number of employees, tools value (£3,500).
  2. Get quotes for correct limits: Choose public liability £2m for domestic, £5m if main contractors require it. Add employers' liability if you employ – minimum £5m legally required – VERIFIED.
  3. Declare height, heat, depth: Declare max height e.g. 12m, excavation depth e.g. 1.5m, heat work yes/no. Non-declaration = claim denial.
  4. Buy and retain Employers' Liability Certificate: If you employ, you must display certificate and retain for 40 years under HSE guidance – VERIFIED via HSE guidance.
  5. Keep safety records: Method statements, risk assessments, hot works permit. Insurers and HSE inspectors ask for them after incident under Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974.
  6. If claim happens: Notify insurer within 24-48 hours (policy condition), collect photos, witness details, keep damaged items, do not admit liability verbally.
  7. Settlement: Insurer handles third-party negotiation, pays up to limit less excess (e.g., £250 PL excess typical – VARIABLE).

Factors Affecting Tradesman Insurance Cost UK

Trade Type & Risk Rating

Roofer, scaffolder, groundworker = higher risk for falls, structural collapse. Painter/decorator = lower. A roofer working at 15m in Glasgow may pay 1.8x – 2.5x more than a painter in Leeds for same £2m limit – HYPOTHETICAL ratio illustration. Insurers rate via ABI trade codes.

  • Height, depth, heat work undeclared = higher or declined
  • Gas Safe Register for gas engineers, NICEIC/ECS for electricians lowers risk? Actually increases if not qualified – factors = certifications
  • Claims history 3-5 years – one public liability claim £3k may increase renewal 20-35% illustrative
Turnover, Employees, Tools & Location

Turnover £30k vs £120k changes exposure. Employees trigger compulsory employers' liability. Tools value £1k vs £8k. Location: London postcode may be higher theft premium than rural Cumbria.

  • Employers' liability: 1 employee adds illustrative £15-£30/month – VARIABLE
  • Tools £5k adds illustrative £8-£18/month – depends on van security
  • Contract works £50k adds more than £10k works – sum insured key
  • Excess choice: raising excess £250 to £500 can cut premium ~10-15% but you pay more on claim

You must declare accurately. You pay based on what you declare. If you under-declare turnover to save £12/month, insurer may apply average condition and pay less at claim. That is more expensive. You need to review yearly.

Business vs Domestic: Commercial Cover vs Personal Use

A lot of confusion happens here. Can your personal liability or home insurance cover trade work at your home? No. If you are repairing your mate's fence for free on Sunday, personal liability might respond. If you charge £200 for that job as a tradesman, you need trade-rated cover. Commercial insurance is different again.

For example, if you search for a business vehicle insurance or commercial auto insurance quote for your van – that is motor insurance covering business use and carriage of tools – it does not cover public liability. You need both: van insurance for driving, tradesman insurance for trade liability. Many think one covers the other. It does not. Not all insurers cover tools in van under tradesman tools section and motor section simultaneously – check overlap.

Similarly, a domestic handyman doing occasional odd jobs for neighbours for cash still has commercial exposure. If you drill through a pipe and flood a kitchen causing £11,000 water damage – HYPOTHETICAL amount – domestic home insurance will not pay because it was business activity. You need tradesman public liability. That is the gap competitors miss.

How to Get Cheapest Tradesman Insurance – 6 Practical Ways to Save

You want cheap, but cheap with gaps costs more. You can lower cost legally without losing cover. Here is how.

  1. Pick correct trade code and limit, not higher: If you are carpenter doing kitchens, don't select builder + roofer if you never roof. Extra trades add ~£10-£25/month illustrative (VARIABLE). If client needs £2m PL, don't buy £5m just in case unless contract requires. £5m can be 25-40% more than £2m for same trade – HYPOTHETICAL.
  2. Increase voluntary excess sensibly: Raising PL excess from £250 to £500 may cut annual premium by £45-£90 illustrative (VARIABLE, not guaranteed) for £60k turnover trader. Keep excess affordable – you pay it on claim.
  3. Annual payment vs monthly: Monthly instalments often include APR 12-20% illustrative via premium finance. Paying annually on £480 premium saves ~£55-£90 in interest – CALCULATED illustrative, not official, check provider.
  4. Combine policies, avoid duplication: Buying PL, EL and tools separately from three insurers often costs more than one tradesman package. Bundle via one broker like Simply Business panel vs direct separate may save £80-£150/year illustrative – HYPOTHETICAL scenario, verify quotes.
  5. Improve van security and document it: Fit Thatcham-approved alarm, deadlocks, tool safe. Photo evidence. Some insurers give 5-10% discount on tools section and lower excess from £250 to £150. Also reduces theft risk, which affects renewal.
  6. Keep turnover accurate and maintain safety records: Accurate turnover prevents penalty at claim. Completing a brief risk assessment and keeping hot-work log shows underwriter lower risk. No official discount guarantee, but claims history clean 3 years often reduces premium 15-25% illustrative vs one claim in last 2 years – VARIABLE by insurer.

Not all insurers cover all money-saving options. Cost varies by postcode, trade and claims. Check your policy wording for minimum security conditions before you assume discount.

Tradesman Insurance Cost UK FAQ – Real Questions

How much does public liability for a tradesman cost per month in the UK?

Is tradesman insurance a legal requirement in the UK?

Public liability is not legally compulsory, but most sites require it contractually. Employers' liability is legally compulsory if you employ staff in Great Britain under Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 with minimum £5m cover – VERIFIED via gov.uk and HSE. You must display certificate and keep 40 years.

What affects tradesman insurance cost the most?

Trade type (height/heat/structural), public liability limit (£1m/£2m/£5m), whether you have employees (triggers EL), turnover, tools value, contract works sum, claims history and postcode. For example, adding one labourer may add £15-£30/month illustrative. All VARIABLE – not guaranteed.

Can I get tradesman insurance for one day or one job?

Some brokers offer short-term public liability for 1-14 days, but most annual policies are cheaper if you work regularly. Daily PL illustrative range £25-£60/day for £1m limit (VARIABLE, check broker). If you do 3+ jobs a month, annual is usually better value.

Does tradesman insurance cover tools theft from van?

Optional add-on covers tools up to sum e.g. £3k-£8k, but requires conditions: van locked, alarmed, tools hidden, evidence of forcible entry, often no cover 9pm-6am unless in locked garage. Not all insurers cover overnight van storage – check wording. Excess £100-£250 typical VARIABLE.

What is the difference between public liability and employers' liability cost?

Public liability pays third party injury/property damage; employers' liability pays employee injury. EL legally required minimum £5m if you employ. Cost illustration observed 15 Aug 2026: PL £2m alone ~£13-£45/month; adding EL for 1 employee increases total to ~£28-£65/month VARIABLE.
