What Is Employers' Liability Insurance?

Employers' liability insurance — often called EL — protects you if an employee is injured or becomes ill because of work for you. It pays legal defence and compensation, not wages. Think of it as the safety net that stops one accident from bankrupting your business. Short answer, then detail. Is it public liability? No. Public covers third parties. EL covers your own people.

You must buy EL from an insurer that appears on the Financial Conduct Authority register. GOV.UK and HSE guidance are clear: a general business bank account, a trade association membership, or a personal accident policy does NOT satisfy Employers Liability Insurance Requirements UK. I learned about this the hard way when reviewing files — many new directors assume their public liability certificate covers employees. It never does. That confusion is expensive.

Under the Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Regulations 1998, your policy must cover at least £5 million for any one occurrence. In reality, almost all UK commercial insurers now issue £10 million as standard for SMEs. Why? Reinsurance pricing makes £10m only slightly more expensive than £5m. So you often get double the protection for a small uplift.

Who Needs It and Why Is It Legally Required?

The dominant search intent here is legal/requirements. You are asking: do I legally need EL? Answer that first. If you have anyone working under your control, direction, and you provide tools, hours, or they cannot send a substitute freely — they likely count as an employee for EL purposes, even if you pay them via CIS or as self-employed. The Health and Safety Executive (HSE) enforces this, not your broker.

You must have EL even for short contracts. Christmas temp? Yes. Work experience student paid expenses only? Likely yes if under your supervision. HMRC status and EL status differ. Do not rely on one to judge the other.

Checklist: Who Counts as Employee for Employers Liability Insurance Requirements UK
  • Full-time and part-time staff on payroll — including those on PAYE even for one day
  • Temporary staff, seasonal staff, agency temps while under your supervision — even if agency has its own EL
  • Trainees, apprentices, students on work experience — paid or unpaid if you direct their work
  • Volunteers — if you control their activities, provide equipment, and they cannot freely delegate
  • Labour-only subcontractors — you supply tools, set hours, cannot send substitute — treated as employee
  • Employees based abroad but normally working in Great Britain — short overseas trips still need GB cover
  • Directors with employment contracts — except limited exemption where you are single employee owning ≥50% of company
  • Staff working from home — homeworkers are employees wherever they work if you control the work

HSE leaflet HSE40 lists narrow exemptions — wholly family-owned unincorporated businesses and specific public organisations. If unsure, check GOV.UK guidance and your insurer.

You may be exempt if: your business is not a limited company and all employees are close family members — husband, wife, father, mother, grandfather, grandmother, stepfather, stepmother, son, daughter, grandson, granddaughter, stepson, stepdaughter, brother, sister, half-brother, half-sister. Also exemption: companies where you are the only employee and you own at least 50% of the share capital. Public bodies such as local authorities, police authorities, and health service bodies are exempt because they are backed by state funds. But these exemptions are narrow. Most limited companies with one director plus a secretary still need EL if secretary has a contract. Not all insurers cover that nuance automatically.

What Does Employers Liability Insurance Requirements UK Include?

To meet legal requirements you need specific features, not just any business insurance receipt. You pay for liability to employees, not for their private health. Here is what the compulsory cover actually includes and where SMEs often trip.

What's Included — Legal Minimum and Standard Market Practice

Limits Box: Minimum Legal Limits and What Insurers Typically Issue (2026)
Legal Minimum Under Act
  • £5,000,000 cover for any one occurrence including costs and expenses
  • Insurer must be authorised by FCA — check Financial Services Register
  • No excess that reduces employee compensation — must be 100% recoverable
  • Great Britain territorial limit as minimum — England, Scotland, Wales

  • £10,000,000 any one claim — most brokers default to £10m EL
  • £5,000,000 additional for terrorism often sub-limited but included
  • Certificate of employers' liability insurance with policy number, insurer, dates
  • Automatic cover for 14 days temporary overseas work if GB-based contract
Records You Must Keep
  • Certificate displayed or accessible electronically to all employees
  • Retention for 40 years — insurers may not keep old certificates after system changes
  • Policy schedule showing trade description — must match actual activities
  • Proof of wage roll and number of employees at renewal — auditable

So what does EL pay for? An employee slips on an untreated warehouse floor, suffers a back injury, cannot work for 4 months. Your EL covers compensation for pain and suffering, loss of earnings, rehabilitation costs, plus your legal defence if they sue under common law or breach of statutory duty. It also covers occupational disease — hearing loss, dermatitis, asthma — even if symptoms appear years later. This is why 40-year certificate retention matters. Diseases can have long latency.

Variable pricing depends heavily on business risk profile: risks like turnover, payroll size, claims history, postcode crime rate, flood risk zone, limit chosen, and excess structure (though excess cannot affect employee). For example, a retail shop in central Nottingham with high public footfall and manual handling will cost more than a two-person accountancy practice in Bath, even with same £10m limit. Turnover £600k with payroll £140k versus turnover £120k with payroll £35k — big difference.

Optional Coverages Often Bought Alongside to Fully Protect Business

EL alone does not cover your stock, your van, or your client who trips. Most employers buy additional covers in a combined policy to avoid gaps. These are optional legally but essential commercially.

  • Public liability insurance — covers injury to customers, members of public, or damage to their property — often £1m to £5m limit. Clients often demand proof.
  • Professional indemnity / products liability — if you give advice or sell products that could cause loss.
  • Contractors all risks, tools, stock — theft, fire, accidental damage — especially relevant for trades but also offices with laptops.
  • Business interruption and loss of rent — if premises become unusable after fire, flood, theft.
  • Personal accident and directors and officers — sick pay gaps and legal claims against directors.
Commercial vs Personal / Free vs Paid / Basic vs Pro — Where CPC Gets Expensive

Free or personal solutions — such as relying on a homeowner policy, personal accident cover, or assuming a holding company protects you — do NOT meet Employers Liability Insurance Requirements UK for a business with employees. A free template certificate or an old public liability PDF will fail HSE inspection. Basic EL at £5m meets law, but Pro bundled with public liability, tools, and business interruption is what commercial contracts, landlords, and clients require. If you also run vehicles, you may compare a commercial auto insurance quote or broader business vehicle insurance alongside your EL to keep compliance costs aligned. That comparison shows why AdSense values commercial-intent searches higher — commercial policies bundle multiple covers into one annual premium rather than a free workaround.

What Is NOT Covered and Common Compliance Mistakes That Cost You

Gap coverage is where I see this mistake daily — employers assume one certificate covers everything. It does not. EL never covers your own injury if you are sole trader with no employees. It never covers motor liability requiring road traffic act insurance. It does not cover deliberate self-harm or injuries sustained while off-duty. And crucially, it does not cover bona fide subcontractors running their own independent business with their own EL and public liability — but if you misclassify them, HSE will treat them as your employees anyway.

🚨 DANGER — 6 Compliance Gaps That Lead to Fines or Unpaid Claims
  • Wrong trade description: Policy says clerical, but staff do manual handling in Leeds warehouse postcode LS12 — claim void for non-disclosure.
  • Labour-only subcontractors declared as bona fide: Pays CIS but you set hours and provide van — counted as employee at claim.
  • No certificate access: Certificate stored only on owner's phone, not accessible to weekend staff — HSE can fine £1,000 for failure to make available.
  • Offshore or overseas work not declared: Standard EL Great Britain only — two-week job in Belfast? Northern Ireland has equivalent law but your GB insurer may need extension for Channel Islands or offshore wind tasks.
  • £5m limit but client requires £10m: You are legal but in breach of contract — construction sites in Birmingham often require £10m EL in tender documents.
  • Payroll underestimated to cut cost: Declared wage roll £55k, actual £145k — insurer reduces claim proportionally for underinsurance. Turnover and payroll audit matters.

Another costly gap: family exemption misread. A limited company with two directors who are brothers and one non-family employee is NOT exempt. The moment one non-family employee exists, EL required. Check your Articles and shareholder register — 50% shareholding test matters for single-employee companies.

How Does It Work? How to Meet Employers Liability Insurance Requirements UK Step by Step

Meeting requirements is not just buying. You must buy correctly, display, and retain. You need a clear order to avoid HSE questions.

✅ SUCCESS — Correct Order to Become Compliant (2026)
  1. Identify employees for EL purposes: List all PAYE staff, temps, trainees, labour-only subcontractors under your control — even one-day staff.
  2. Calculate wage roll and duties: Total payroll, split clerical vs manual, any work at height, use of heat, chemicals. Be accurate — claims postcode, flood zone, and crime rate also affect premium.
  3. Choose FCA-authorised insurer with £5m minimum (usually £10m): Check Financial Services Register, ensure policy wording includes Employers Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act compliance clause.
  4. Get certificate, check details: Policy number, insurer name, cover dates, trade description, £5m+ limit. Ensure Great Britain territorial limit covers your work.
  5. Display or make electronically accessible: Pinprinted copy in reception or upload to intranet, staff handbook app, or shared drive where all can view — not just head office.
  6. Retain for 40 years: Store digital PDFs in secure long-term storage — Dropbox alone not enough — insurers may purge after 6 years.
  7. Review at each hire or trade change: New employee in Edinburgh branch increases wage roll — notify insurer mid-term if material increase, otherwise risk underinsurance.

FAQ: Employers Liability Insurance Requirements UK 2026

Is employers' liability insurance compulsory in the UK?

Yes, in Great Britain under the Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 and 1998 Regulations. Most employers must have at least £5 million cover from an FCA-authorised insurer, display the certificate where employees can access it, and keep it for 40 years. Narrow exemptions exist for wholly family-owned unincorporated businesses and certain public bodies. HSE enforces.

What are the exemptions to employers' liability insurance requirements UK?

Exempt: unincorporated businesses where all employees are close family, companies where you are the only employee and own ≥50% of shares, and most public organisations like health service bodies and local authorities. Family definition is strict — siblings, parents, children, etc. If you employ anyone outside that, exemption ends.

Do I need EL for subcontractors, freelancers or volunteers?

It depends on control. If you supervise them, set hours, provide tools, and they cannot send a substitute, they are likely labour-only subcontractors and need EL under your policy despite self-employed tax status. Bona fide subcontractors running their own business with their own insurance and public liability do not usually need cover from you. Volunteers under your direction often do.

What is the minimum cover — £5m or £10m?

The legal minimum is £5 million any one occurrence including costs. Market practice in 2026 is £10 million as standard for SMEs because reinsurance makes price difference small — often £15-£40 illustrative variable extra. Check client contracts, many construction tenders require £10m EL in Birmingham, Manchester, London.

What fines can I get if I don't have employers' liability insurance?

HSE can fine up to £2,500 for each day you are without suitable cover and impose £1,000 for failing to display or make the certificate available. If an employee is injured while you are uninsured, you must pay compensation and legal costs personally — claims often exceed £20,000, sometimes £100,000+.

How long must I keep my employers' liability certificate?

40 years. This is written into GOV.UK guidance because industrial diseases like mesothelioma can appear decades later. Insurers may only retain records 6 years. Save PDFs in long-term secure storage — e.g., two locations — and ensure new owners get historic certificates if you buy a business with employees.

Does employers' liability insurance cover sole traders with no staff?

No. If you are a genuine sole trader with no employees, labour-only subcontractors, or trainees, you do not need EL — you need sole-trader-insurance for public liability and tools. But the moment you hire one person, even one-day temp in Glasgow or Cardiff, EL becomes compulsory. Check small-business-insurance for combined options.
