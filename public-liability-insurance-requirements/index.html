What Is Public Liability Insurance Requirements UK?

Public liability insurance is not a law like employers' liability, it's a contract standard. In simple terms, it protects you if a member of the public is injured or their property is damaged because of your business. The "requirements" part is what your client, venue, local council, or trade body tells you must be in place before they let you start work.

Think of it as two layers. The legal layer: you are responsible under common law for duty of care. The commercial layer: to manage that risk, almost every contractor agreement adds a clause – "Contractor must hold PL insurance of at least £X million and provide proof before commencement." No proof, no start.

I see this mistake daily – and that phrasing matters because it shows a gap in understanding. Many sole traders buy the cheapest £1m policy online without checking the trade wording. Then a letting agent in Manchester asks for £5m and a specific activities clause, and their policy is rejected on the spot.

Who Needs Public Liability Insurance? Why Is It Required?

Public liability isn't mandatory by statute, yet it is practically mandatory if you want to get paid. If you visit client premises, work in public spaces, or handle other people's property, a contract will require it. Even market stalls in Leeds City Council markets must show £5m now before a pitch is allocated.

  • ✔ Are a self-employed tradesman – electrician, plumber, builder, roofer, painter
  • ✔ Work as a contractor or subcontractor on construction sites (CSCS, JIB, NICEIC requirements)
  • ✔ Run a cleaning, gardening, dog-walking, beauty, or mobile catering business with public contact

  • ✔ Tender for local council or government work where £5m or £10m is written into the tender document

What Does Public Liability Insurance Cover? Requirements & Limits

Coverage itself is straightforward. What matters for requirements is the detail behind the certificate – the limit of indemnity, the exact trade description, and the territorial wording. A client in Bristol won't accept a policy that says "handyman" if the contract says "electrical installation".

What's Included – Standard Requirements

Limits Box – What Clients Check (3-Point Validation)

1. Limit of Indemnity

£1m minimum for low-risk, £2m standard for trades, £5m for electricians / contractors / roofers. Council tenders often demand £5m-£10m. Must be per claim, not aggregate, unless contract accepts aggregate.

2. Business Description & Activities

Must match actual work. "Electrician including testing & inspection" vs generic "property maintenance" matters. Hot work, working at height over 10m, and depth over 2m often need explicit endorsement – otherwise claim rejected even with high limit.

3. Proof & Territorial / Period

Certificate must show business name, address, period of insurance, insurer, policy number. UK territorial limits (including Northern Ireland) and cover for premises, client sites, and public spaces. Some venues require 30 days notice of cancellation clause.

All limits are VARIABLE and depend on trade risk, insurer, and contract. Check your specific contract wording.

Standard public liability in the UK typically pays legal defence costs plus compensation if you are found liable for accidental third-party bodily injury or property damage. Example: you spill adhesive on a client's oak floor in Cardiff, or a passer-by trips over your cable in Newcastle and breaks a wrist.

Optional Coverages Often Required by Contract

  • Products Liability: if you supply, install, orrepair items that later cause injury. Often bundled, but some clients demand it separately at £2m.
  • Financial Loss / Efficacy: for electricians and heating engineers where faulty work causes loss without physical damage.
  • Contract Works / JCT clause: for contractors under JCT contracts – client may require £2m PL plus contractors all-risks.
  • Height & Depth cover: standard policies exclude work above 10m or below 2m unless declared. Scaffolders and roofers must add it.
  • Hot work endorsement: required if using blowtorches, welding. Must declare; otherwise invalid.

What Is NOT Covered? 6 Costly Exclusions Contractors Miss

This is where most trades lose money. They meet the headline limit but miss exclusions buried in the key facts. Your client will still hold you liable, but your insurer won't pay. That gap is personal debt.

🚨 Danger Box – Do NOT Assume These Are Covered

  1. Your own injury or your employees – public liability never covers you or staff. You need employers' liability (£5m legal minimum) and personal accident. HSE can fine you up to £2,500 per day for no EL if you employ.
  2. Defective workmanship – PL pays for resulting damage, not re-doing your faulty work. If an electrician wires a circuit wrongly, the re-wire cost is excluded; the fire damage to client property may be covered.
  3. Professional advice / design – if you design a system and it fails, you need professional indemnity. A plumber specifying boiler size is professional advice, not manual work.
  4. Tools, vans, plant – public liability does not cover theft of tools or van damage. Need separate tools cover (£1k-£15k typical) and commercial vehicle insurance. A common expensive confusion.
  5. Work at undeclared height, depth, or hot work – many £2m tradesman policies automatically exclude over 10m height, under 2m depth, or any heat work. Claims denied if not endorsed, even if within limit.
  6. Pollution, asbestos, and gradual damage – gradual leak over months, asbestos removal, or pollution events are excluded unless specialist endorsement. Electricians pulling old boards with asbestos risk must declare.

How Does It Work? How to Meet Requirements Step-by-Step

Meeting the requirements is not just buying a policy. It's matching paperwork to contract. Clients don't read your whole policy – they read your certificate and schedule. If wording doesn't mirror their contract, they reject.

✅ Success Box – 6 Steps to Satisfy Any UK Client

  1. Read the clause first: Find the insurance clause in your contract / venue form. Note limit (e.g., £5m), required extensions (products, hot work), and any named interested party.
  2. Match trade description: Call insurer to set exact HMRC trade: e.g., "Domestic and commercial electrician including inspection & testing and EV charger install, excluding renewable solar over 10kW unless endorsed."
  3. Choose correct indemnity: £2m is cheapest but often insufficient. For electricians, contractors, roofers – select £5m. For council/government tenders, ask if £10m is needed.
  4. Get certificate with right name: Ensure business legal name matches Companies House or HMRC self-employed name. If trading as Ltd, certificate must show Ltd name, not personal name.
  5. Request endorsement proof: If you need height, depth, hot work, or JCT clause, ask for schedule showing "Height limit 15m included" – keep as PDF for clients.
  6. Submit & keep renewal reminder: Send certificate, schedule, and EL certificate if employing. Set reminder 14 days before renewal – many clients auto-suspend if insurance lapses even for one day.

⚠️ Warning Box – Common Requirement Failure

The most common mistake I see: buying cover in the wrong business name. If you are "J Smith Electrical Ltd" but certificate says "John Smith" (sole trader), the principal contractor's compliance portal (e.g., Constructionline, CHAS) will auto-fail you. Always ensure Companies House number and trading name match exactly. Also check if your policy includes "labour-only subcontractors" – many cheap policies exclude them, which immediately breaches contractor requirements.

Commercial vs Personal: Why Regular Insurance Won't Meet Requirements

Personal liability add-ons from home insurance, or free liability included with a bank account, never meet trade contract requirements. Those covers are for private life – e.g., your dog bites someone in the park. They exclude any work activity, fee-earning, or business at client premises.

If you also drive a van with tools, many traders think van insurance covers public liability. It doesn't. You still need separate public liability, and you also need business class van insurance. If you carry goods for others, getting a separate commercial auto insurance quote is worth reviewing as a parallel cover, but it never replaces the PL certificate your client asks for. One is motor risk, one is public risk – clients require both.

In short: Basic or free cover = personal, no contract value, claims denied for work. Commercial public liability = correct trade, correct limit, endorsements, certificate accepted by compliance teams. Don't mix them – a rejection delays payment.

Factors That Increase or Decrease Your Requirement Cost

  • Trade risk: Electrician, roofer, scaffolder pays 1.8x-3x a cleaner or tutor at same £2m limit due to injury/fire risk.
  • Turnover & employee numbers: £50k turnover sole trader cheaper than £350k Ltd with 3 labour-only subcontractors – more exposure = higher premium.

  • History & claims: One claim in last 3 years can inflate by 25-60%. No claims, clean history reduces.
  • Height / depth / hot work: Adding 15m height or hot work often +£60-£150/year but mandatory for acceptance – omission risks voidance.
  • Payment monthly vs annual: Monthly direct debit adds ~8-12% APR. Paying annually saves £18-£40 on £200 base.

How to Get the Cheapest Cover That Still Meets Requirements

Cheapest doesn't mean lowest price – it means lowest price that still passes compliance. Buying £1m when contract demands £5m is wasted £58. Here are 6 practical tips with real numbers:

FAQ – Public Liability Insurance Requirements UK

Is public liability insurance legally required in the UK?

No. Unlike employers' liability (legally required £5m minimum), public liability is not required by law. However, it is practically required by client contracts, venues, market licences, mortgage lenders for trades, and trade bodies like NICEIC and Gas Safe. Without it you cannot start most jobs.

What limit do I need – £1m, £2m or £5m for contractors?

£1m is minimum for low-risk self-employed (tutor, beauty). £2m is standard for most tradesman insurance requirements (plumber, joiner). £5m is demanded for electrician insurance requirements, roofing, scaffolding, and for contractor insurance requirements on commercial sites and council tenders. Always read your contract.

What documents prove I meet requirements?

You need a certificate of insurance showing insurer, policy number, business name, trade, period, limit, and schedule showing endorsements (height, hot work, products). Some clients also ask for employers' liability certificate and proof of professional indemnity if you design.

Do I need public liability if I work from home with no visitors?

If you never visit clients and no public visits you, some insurers say low risk. However, if you visit even one client per year, or sell a product that could injure, requirements still apply. Online sellers still need products liability, often insisted on by marketplaces.

Does public liability cover subcontractors?

Only if policy includes "labour-only subcontractors" or "bona-fide subcontractors" clause. Many cheap £1m policies exclude them. For contractor insurance requirements, you must ensure your policy extends to acts of subcontractors, otherwise you breach contract and face personal liability for their mistake.

How fast can I get a certificate that meets requirements?

Most UK brokers issue same-day PDF after online purchase – AXA, Hiscox, and Simply Business typically email within 30 minutes. If you need specific wording like "principal indemnity" or named client as interested party, allow 24-48 hours for broker endorsement.
