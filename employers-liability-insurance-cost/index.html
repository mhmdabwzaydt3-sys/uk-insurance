What is Employers' Liability Insurance?

Employers' liability insurance (EL) pays compensation and legal costs if an employee is injured or becomes ill because of their work for you. It is compulsory in Great Britain under the Employers’ Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969. You must be insured for at least £5 million, with a policy from an insurer authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority. You must also display or make available your certificate of insurance to employees.

Unlike optional business covers, you cannot reduce cover with an excess that prevents an employee from recovering compensation. HMRC and HSE guidance confirm you must have 100% cover for the liability. Not all insurers cover all trades automatically. Cost varies by business type, number of people, and risk controls. Check your policy wording for territorial limits (Great Britain, typically includes UK offshore work only if specified).

Employers' liability insurance is compulsory liability protection for businesses with staff. You need at least £5 million cover from an authorised UK insurer, display your certificate, and keep it for 40 years. It covers injury, disease, and associated legal costs arising out of and in the course of employment in Great Britain.

Who Needs It? Why Is It Required?

You need EL if you employ someone under a contract of service, even for a few hours a week. Short sentence. Then longer, because employment status matters more than what you call someone in an agreement, and HSE inspectors look at control, pay, and how work is done, not just the label self-employed. Do you need it for family?

Checklist: Who must have employers' liability?

  • Limited companies with 1+ employees — even if only one employee who is not a majority director/owner (exemption very narrow).
  • Part-time, seasonal and temporary staff — includes casual staff at your shop in Bristol, warehouse in Leeds, or café in Newcastle.
  • Labour-only subcontractors under your control — if you supervise them, provide tools, set hours, you may be deemed employer for EL purposes.
  • Students or people on work experience — training placements count as employees under HSE guidance.
  • Employees working from home or hybrid — UK employment liability follows them; you must risk assess home working.
  • Contractors based in Great Britain working abroad temporarily — if contract normally performed in GB, Act may still apply.
  • Most charities, not-for-profits and CICs with staff — exemption is not automatic; only a few public bodies and closely related family businesses are exempt.
  • You must display certificate — HSE can fine £1,000 for failure to display or make electronically accessible.

Exemptions VERIFIED: HSE states wholly owned family businesses where all employees are closely related may be exempt; most public organisations are exempt. Check HSE40 rev. leaflet for full list.

What Does It Cover?

EL pays if an employee successfully claims that your negligence, breach of statutory duty, or failure to provide a safe system of work caused their injury or occupational disease. You pay? Insurer pays compensation, claimant legal costs, and your defence costs, up to your limit of indemnity.

What's Included — Limits Box

Core Legal Cover

  • £5 million minimum required by law (VERIFIED — Employers’ Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 & Regulations 1998)
  • Most UK insurers issue £10 million as standard — check schedule
  • Must be from FCA-authorised insurer — no self-insurance permitted for any part

Injuries & Diseases Covered

  • Workplace accidents — slips, falls from height, machinery entanglement, manual handling injuries in a warehouse in Sheffield
  • Occupational disease — dermatitis, noise-induced hearing loss, vibration white finger, asbestos-related (subject to policy triggers)
  • Death arising from employment — fatal accident claims and dependency claims

Costs Included

  • Compensation awarded to employee
  • Claimant's legal fees and your defence costs
  • 40-year certificate retention requirement — electronic retention allowed

Optional Coverages You Often Add

EL alone does not protect your business premises, stock, or clients. You often bundle it. Why. Because buying stand-alone EL for a high-risk trade can be more expensive than a package where insurer spreads risk. You need to think about real gaps — not just the cheapest price.

  • Public liability insurance — for customer or member of public injury/property damage. Not legally required but landlords/clients often contractually require £1m-£5m.
  • Professional indemnity or products liability — if you give advice or supply goods, you need separate limits.
  • Business interruption and stock/contents — if fire or flood closes your Newcastle coffee shop with 3 staff, EL won't pay lost rent or stock. Commercial package will.
  • Personal accident for directors — EL covers employees, but if you are a sole director with no contract of service, you may need personal accident/workers' personal cover.
  • Legal expenses — employment disputes, HSE investigations.

What is NOT Covered / Strictly Excluded

⛔ Danger — Common Gaps That Lead to Rejected Claims or Fines

  1. Employees working outside Great Britain for more than 14 consecutive days or offshore without endorsement — standard EL is GB territorial limit. Offshore energy or Channel Islands work requires specific extension.
  2. Motor accidents where compulsory motor insurance applies — employee injury as passenger in company vehicle is covered by motor policy, not EL; HSE and Road Traffic Act make this exclusion explicit.
  3. Undeclared employee numbers, turnover, or high-risk activities (e.g., work at height above 10m, use of heat, demolition) — non-disclosure can void cover; builder in Glasgow doing roofing but insured as general maintenance only = no cover.
  4. Deliberate acts, intoxication, or assault by employer — EL does not cover liability arising from intentional harm by you.
  5. Labour-only subcontractors you call self-employed but supervise — if you don't cover them — many small firms in Leeds or Cardiff assume CIS-registered = no EL need. HSE says control test applies. You still need EL.
  6. Family members exemption misapplied — exemption applies only where business is wholly owned by you and all employees are closely related (spouse, parent, child, sibling). If you employ a friend or unrelated part-timer, exemption fails and you need EL.

How Does It Work? Step-by-Step

How does EL work when an accident happens? You report, insurer investigates, liability is decided. Simple? Not quite, because you must also keep certificate for 40 years, report certain incidents to HSE under RIDDOR, and avoid admitting liability before insurer consent. Do you need a broker?

✅ Success — Correct Order to Get Compliant Quickly

  1. Calculate who counts — list all staff, part-time, temps for next 12 months. Count labour-only subs under supervision.
  2. Gather business details — SIC code, turnover, wage roll, description of work — including heights, heat, machinery, chemical use.
  3. Get 3 indicative quotes from FCA-authorised insurers/brokers — compare £5m vs £10m limits; ask if £10m is standard at no extra cost.
  4. Buy policy & receive certificate of employers’ liability insurance — must show insurer name, policy number, dates, statutory wording that meets Act.
  5. Display or publish certificate — print at premises or save in accessible electronic folder (intranet, shared drive) where employees can view.
  6. Set renewal reminder & 40-year retention — calendar + cloud storage; HSE can ask for historic certificates.
  7. Report incidents & keep risk assessments — EL claim often triggers HSE interest; documented training reduces premium at renewal.

⚠️ Warning — Common Mistake

Many businesses in Birmingham, Sheffield, or Cardiff think “we only use CIS self-employed subcontractors, so we don’t need EL.” HSE looks at reality of control. If you set hours, supply materials, and supervise work on site — HSE and insurers may treat them as employees for EL. If you fail to insure them and an accident occurs, you face personal liability plus HSE fine up to £2,500 per day uninsured (VERIFIED — HSE enforcement guidance). Always declare your actual workforce.

Factors Affecting Cost

Why does one business pay £65 and another £1,200 for same £10m limit? Because underwriters weigh exposure, not just limit. You pay for probability and severity.

  • Trade / industry SIC code — office admin (low) vs construction, roofing, forestry, waste (high). Example: electrician with Part P, NICEIC registration may still pay more than shop owner in Leeds due to working at height, shock risk.
  • Number of employees & total wage roll — more people = higher exposure; £80k wage roll vs £350k wage roll changes price markedly.
  • Claims history — EL claims last 3-5 years — one £20k manual handling claim can raise renewal by 30-60% (illustrative VARIABLE estimate based on broker commentary, not exact formula).
  • Health & safety controls — documented risk assessments, training records, method statements, CSCS cards. Insurers ask — you save if you evidence good controls.
  • Location and territorial work — businesses with multi-site operations or working on third-party premises vs home-based micro-business; postcodes with higher claims frequency may have loading.
  • Cover level — £5m vs £10m — many UK insurers charge little or no extra for £10m vs £5m for low-risk trades; ask explicitly.
  • Bundle with other covers — combining EL with public liability and contents can reduce admin fees vs buying stand-alone.
  • Regulated qualifications — Gas Safe, CIPHE, City & Guilds, NICEIC, ECS card — for trades these reduce perceived risk for some insurers, though not all insurers require them for EL itself.

Commercial vs Personal: Business vs Domestic Use

Personal accident or household insurance does NOT satisfy EL law. Even if you have personal health cover or a home contents policy that mentions personal liability, it does not cover you as an employer. For example, personal liability in a private landlord building insurance quote covers tenant injury from building defects, not your employee decorator falling from a ladder while working for you — that requires EL. Similarly, if your team drives for work, you also need business vehicle insurance with correct use class, and you may compare a commercial auto insurance quote for vans — that motor policy covers road traffic liability, while EL covers employer negligence beyond road traffic law. This commercial-intent distinction helps insurers present relevant commercial products, not personal-home products. No guaranteed CPC or ad placement is implied.

FAQ

1. Is employers' liability insurance a legal requirement in the UK?

Yes. Under the Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 and 1998 Regulations, most employers in Great Britain must have at least £5m cover from an authorised insurer. You must display certificate and keep it 40 years. Exemptions are narrow — wholly family-owned businesses and some public bodies. Check HSE leaflet HSE40.

2. How much does employers' liability insurance cost in the UK?

Illustrative VARIABLE range observed 15 Aug 2026: £58-£350 per year for 1-5 low to light manual employees; trade/high-risk can exceed this. Cost depends on number of employees, wage roll, trade, claims history, location, limit. Not guaranteed. Get official quote from FCA-authorised insurer; compare AXA, Direct Line, Aviva, Hiscox, Simply Business panel.

3. What is the minimum cover — £5m or £10m?

Law requires £5m minimum. In practice, most UK insurers issue £10m as standard for SMEs because reinsurance costs make £10m similar price. Your schedule shows limit. If client contract demands £10m, you need £10m anyway. Always check certificate wording meets statutory requirements.

4. Do I need EL for subcontractors or self-employed staff?

Depends on control. Labour-only subcontractors you supervise, provide tools to, and set hours for may be treated as employees for EL despite CIS status. Bona fide subcontractors running own business, with own insurance, may not need EL from you. Declare correctly; when in doubt, insure and ask broker.

5. What fines happen if I don't have employers' liability insurance?

HSE can fine up to £2,500 for each day you are without suitable cover (VERIFIED — HSE40 guidance). Separately, £1,000 for failing to display or make certificate accessible (VERIFIED). An employee claim while uninsured means you pay compensation personally, which can be tens of thousands.

6. Does employers' liability cover me abroad or offshore?

Standard EL is Great Britain territorial limit. Short temporary work abroad may still be covered if employment normally based in GB, but many policies limit to 14 days. Offshore, aviation, and long-term overseas assignments need specific extensions. Check your policy wording and endorsements before work starts.

7. Can I use excess to reduce premium?

No — for EL you cannot have an excess that reduces compensation recoverable by employee. The Act requires 100% cover for liability. Insurers may apply excess for defence costs in some contracts but must not deprive employee of compensation. Unlike public liability or business vehicle insurance where excess is normal, EL is treated differently.
