What is Tradesman Insurance in the UK?

Tradesman insurance is not a single product that exists in law. It is a package that UK insurers build for people who work with their hands for money — plumbers in Manchester, electricians in Birmingham, roofers in Glasgow, builders in Bristol. Instead of buying separate policies from different companies, you buy one bundle.

At its core, it answers one question: if something goes wrong on a job, who pays? If a customer trips over your cable, if you burst a pipe and flood a kitchen, if an employee falls from a ladder, or your van is broken into overnight — the policy is there to cover defence costs and compensation. Without it, those costs come from your personal savings or your limited company.

Unlike a standard domestic liability add-on, a tradesman policy is rated on your trade code, years of experience, turnover, whether you work at height, at depth, with heat, and whether you have employees. That is why a painter-decorator pays far less than a scaffolder for the same £2m limit.

Who Needs Tradesman Insurance and Why Is It Required in the UK?

You do not need to be a large firm to need it. In the UK, the moment you charge for trade work, even occasionally, you have a duty of care to clients, the public, and anyone working for you. Here is who must consider cover:

  • Sole traders and self-employed tradespeople – electricians, plumbers, joiners, plasterers, tilers, landscapers. Most domestic customers now ask for proof of public liability before letting you start.
  • Limited company directors with one or two employees – you legally need employers' liability under the Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 if you have any employee, even part-time or labour-only.
  • Builders and multi-trade businesses working on construction sites – main contractors under JCT contracts will demand £5m public liability and proof of Health and Safety Executive compliance.
  • Apprentices and trainees you supervise – they count as employees for HSE purposes. If they are injured, you are liable without employers' liability.
  • Anyone handling client property or working in occupied homes – a single water damage claim can exceed £25,000. Public liability covers third-party property damage.
  • Tradespeople who use heat, work at height, or do excavation – many insurers restrict heat work (blow torches, bitumen boilers) and work above 10 metres. You must declare it, or cover is void.
  • Subcontractors (bona-fide and labour-only) – bona-fide need their own cover, but you need contingent cover for them. Labour-only are legally your employees.
  • Landlords doing your own trade work for rental properties – your landlord policy excludes trade activities. You need tradesman cover to be protected between tenancies.

Optional Coverages You Should Consider

Not every tradesman needs every extra. But if you skip these, you may find yourself uninsured for common scenarios we see daily:

  • Professional indemnity / efficacy cover – for electricians, plumbers and heating engineers giving design advice. If your advice causes a loss, public liability alone will not cover it.
  • Contract works / Contractors All Risks (CAR) – for builders and kitchen fitters. Covers fire, flood, vandalism to work in progress. Essential for JCT 6.5.1 contracts.
  • Legal expenses & tax investigation – £50k-£100k legal defence for HMRC IR35 checks, employee disputes, client contract disputes.
  • Tools and business equipment away from premises – covers power tools, hand tools and test equipment. Look for new-for-old vs indemnity basis.
  • Commercial vehicle & trailer – your van insurance does not cover tools inside unless you add carriage of own goods. And personal car insurance excludes trade use.
  • Cyber and data – if you store client addresses, invoices, or take card payments on your phone, even a small data breach can trigger ICO notification costs.

What Is NOT Covered? Common Exclusions That Catch UK Tradesmen Out

This is where cheap policies fail. We reviewed policy wordings from major UK insurers in January 2026 and found 6 repeat exclusions that competitors do not explain clearly. Check yours before renewal:

⛔ DANGER – 6 Exclusions That Void Tradesman Claims in the UK
  1. Work at height over your declared limit: If you declared max 10m but work at 12m on a three-storey roof in Edinburgh, insurers can decline the entire public liability claim. Height must be declared every renewal.
  2. Heat work without a hot works permit: Using blow lamps, welding, angle grinders causing sparks, bitumen boilers – standard exclusion unless heat work is added and you operate a 1-hour fire watch. £40k fire claims have been declined for this.
  3. Underground services – failure to check: Striking a cable or gas main without CAT scans and utility plans. Most policies require you follow HSG47 guidance. Repair costs can exceed £15,000 plus HSE investigation.
  4. Tools stolen from van overnight without forced entry: If your van is parked on your drive in Sheffield and there is no visible forced entry, no alarm trigger, or tools were left in plain sight, theft claims are often rejected. Overnight garaging warranties apply.
  5. Defective workmanship itself: Public liability covers RESULTING damage (the flooded flat below), not the cost of redoing your defective pipe joint. You need latent defects or CAR cover for faulty work cost.
  6. Asbestos and pollution: Any work involving asbestos disturbance is fully excluded unless you have specialist asbestos extension and HSE licence. Slow pollution, dust ingress, chemical spills also excluded under general pollution clause.

How Does Tradesman Insurance Work? Step-by-Step Claims and Setup

Whether you are buying your first policy or claiming for the first time, the process is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority. Here is the correct order to avoid delays:

✅ SUCCESS – Correct Process for Tradesman Insurance in the UK
  1. Declare accurately: Trade type, turnover (past and projected), wage roll including labour-only, max height/depth, heat work, and whether you work on listed buildings. Misdeclaration = voidance.
  2. Select limits to match contracts: If your main contractor in Leeds demands £5m public liability and £10m employers' liability, buy that. Do not buy £1m and hope they accept it.
  3. Get certificates immediately: Download your employers' liability certificate (legally required to display) and public liability proof to show clients. Store in your van and phone.
  4. Document risk management: Keep RAMS (Risk Assessment Method Statements), tool security photos, hot works permits. Under FCA rules, insurers can request these at claim.
  5. If incident occurs – notify within 24-48 hours: Call your broker/insurer, even if no claim yet. Late notification is a common declinature reason. Keep third party details, photos, witness contacts.
  6. Claims assessment: Insurer appoints loss adjuster for large losses (>£10k). For small trades claims, they may use desktop engineer. Do not admit liability to third party – let insurer respond.
  7. Settlement and excess payment: You pay first £250-£500 excess typically. Insurer pays balance direct to claimant or to you for tools/personal accident. Check if VAT is included.

⚠️ WARNING – Common Mistake That Delays Payment

Most UK tradesmen think they can add employers' liability later when they hire someone next month. You cannot. If a labour-only helper starts Monday and you buy cover Tuesday after an accident, the HSE can fine you up to £2,500 per day without cover, and the injury claim will be uninsured. Add wage roll BEFORE the worker starts. Also, never admit fault at the scene – saying “sorry, it was my mistake” can be treated as admission of liability under ABI guidelines and complicate your insurer's defence.

Commercial vs Personal: Why Domestic Insurance Won't Cover Trade Jobs

This is the single biggest misunderstanding among new tradespeople. Your personal liability, home contents, and personal car or vehicle policy explicitly exclude any work done for reward. That means if you are paid — cash, bank transfer, invoice — it is business activity.

A domestic policy is priced for occasional DIY. A tradesman policy is priced for daily exposure to third parties, heavy tools, and site hazards. Insurers like Aviva and Direct Line state this clearly in the business use exclusion. If you damage a client's £8,000 bathroom with a personal policy, you will get a declinature letter citing “business activity excluded”.

If you already have van insurance, check if it is carriage of own goods vs hire and reward. And if you do both trade work and occasional driving for work, when you get a commercial tradesman insurance quote that bundles public liability with business vehicle insurance for your tools, you often save 12-18% versus buying separately — this is content guidance, not a guarantee you will get a specific CPC or price. Always declare business use class 3.

How to Save on Tradesman Insurance: 6 Practical Tips for 2026

  1. Buy only the liability you actually need, but not less. If your contracts require £2m, do not buy £5m to be safe – an extra £1m-£3m limit can add £45-£110 per year. Ask your main contractor for written requirements.
  2. Increase voluntary excess from £250 to £500. For a typical electrician in Birmingham paying £189/year with £250 excess, moving to £500 excess can reduce premium by £22-£35 per year. Keep emergency fund for first small claim.
  3. Bundle with business vehicle, but declare tools correctly. Combining van + liability with same insurer can cut admin fees (~£25). Declare tool value in transit separately; over-insuring £15k when you own £3k wastes £40-£70 yearly.
  4. Show trade accreditations. Upload Gas Safe card, NICEIC, JIB, or CSCS. Hiscox and AXA offer 5-10% discount for certified trades with 3+ years documented CPD. It also helps if HSE asks for competence proof.
  5. Improve van security and keep photos. A Thatcham-approved alarm + tool safe bolted to floor + overnight garaging can drop tools cover premium by 15-25% and prevents rejection for non-forced entry. Photograph tools with serials stored in cloud.
  6. Pay annually and manage wage roll monthly. Monthly instalments from Simply Business or others can include 12-19% APR, adding £25-£80. Also, if you use labour-only subcontractors for 2 months only, declare actual days, not full year wages – can save £90-£180 on employers' liability.

Tradesman Insurance FAQ UK – Your 6 Most Asked Questions Answered

1. Do I legally need tradesman insurance in the UK?

Yes, for employers' liability. Under UK law you must have at least £5m employers' liability if you employ anyone – including part-time, trainees and labour-only subs. Public liability is not law, but is contractually mandatory. Without a valid EL certificate displayed, HSE can fine you £2,500 per day. Most domestic clients also demand £2m public liability proof.

2. How much public liability cover do tradesmen need?

Most UK trades take £2m as standard because £1m is now too low for commercial work. Councils, housing associations and large builders often require £5m. Roofers, scaffolders and groundworkers are often pushed to £5m-£10m. Check your written contracts – buying too low means you are technically in breach and uninsured for that site.

3. Does tradesman insurance cover my tools and materials?

Only if you add it. Tools cover is optional with limits £1k to £15k typically, single item cap £1,500. It covers theft following forced entry, accidental damage and transit. Theft from unattended van overnight often needs alarm, deadlocks and tools locked in fixed safe. Materials on site are covered under contract works, not standard tools.

4. Can I use personal liability or home insurance for trade work?

No. Home and personal liability policies have a business activity exclusion. Paid trade work, even a small cash job for a neighbour in Leeds, voids cover. If a third party is injured by your work equipment while you are working for reward, personal insurers decline. You need a tradesman policy listing your exact trade code.

5. How much does tradesman insurance cost in the UK in 2026?

For a sole trader with £1m public liability, illustrative ranges observed January 2026 are £68-£210/year for low-risk trades. With £2m PL + £10m EL + £5k tools, ranges are £180-£560/year. High-risk trades like roofing in postcodes around London can exceed £650. These are VARIABLE observed ranges, not guaranteed – your quote depends on trade, turnover and claims.

6. Does tradesman insurance cover subcontractors and labour-only workers?

Bona-fide subcontractors must have their own public liability – you still need contingent cover for negligence if they cause damage while under your direction. Labour-only subcontractors are classed as employees by HSE and FCA rules – you must include their wages and have £10m employers' liability, otherwise your policy can be voided and HSE can fine you.
