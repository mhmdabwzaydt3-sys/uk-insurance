It pays gross profit shortfall, fixed expenses, and relocation costs during the indemnity period, helping shops, pubs, and restaurants recover until trading resumes.

What is Business Interruption Insurance UK?

Business Interruption Insurance UK, often called BI or loss of profit insurance, does not fix your building. Your commercial property insurance fixes the building. This cover fixes your cash flow while you cannot trade. When a fire, flood, storm or escape of water damages your premises, your insurer pays for material damage. But who pays your rent, staff salaries, and lost margin while you are closed for three months?

That is the gap this policy fills. In the UK, the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), Association of British Insurers (ABI), Chartered Insurance Institute (CII), and case law from the FCA Business Interruption test case set expectations for how BI must be worded and sold. Under the Insurance Act 2015, you have a duty of fair presentation. You must accurately declare turnover, gross profit, and indemnity period.

Think of it this way: property cover rebuilds the walls. Business interruption rebuilds the income stream inside them.

Business Interruption Insurance UK is crucial for UK SMEs because fixed costs do not stop when trading stops. HMRC still expects PAYE and VAT, landlords still expect rent, and Companies House still expects filings. Without this cover, many businesses never reopen. Industry analysis from the ABI and HSE business continuity guidance shows that lack of interruption cover is a leading cause of permanent closure after a major incident.

Snippet explainer — What counts as business interruption in the UK?

Business Interruption Insurance UK is a commercial insurance that replaces lost gross profit after insured damage to your premises, suppliers, or customers. It requires an active material damage policy, covers fixed costs and increased cost of working during the chosen indemnity period, typically 12-24 months, and excludes non-damage events unless specifically extended.

Who Needs Business Interruption Insurance UK?

If you have a physical location where customers spend money, or fixed contracts that run even when you are closed, you should consider it. The FCA does not make it legally compulsory like employers’ liability, but landlords, lenders, and licensing conditions often require evidence of adequate BI limits.

  • High-street shops and retail in commercial-property-insurance premises with stock and fit-out at risk from fire or water damage.
  • Pubs, bars and nightclubs covered under pub-insurance where loss of licence or cellar flood can stop wet and dry sales for weeks.
  • Restaurants, cafés and takeaways on restaurant-insurance where kitchen fire or equipment breakdown halts cover counts and delivery revenue.
  • Small businesses and micro-SMEs with small-business-insurance packages — many have rent, staff, and loan repayments that continue during closure.
  • Businesses reliant on nearby footfall — e.g., a salon in Manchester Arndale affected by damage to an anchor tenant or blocked access.
  • Manufacturers and wholesalers with single-site production where a machine fire stops all output — even a 4-week delay can breach contracts.
  • Professional services in leased offices — loss of access due to flood in Birmingham city centre can force temporary relocation costs.
  • Landlords requiring continuity — many commercial leases require tenants to maintain BI for 24 months to ensure rent can still be met after damage.

You particularly need it if your gross profit relies on perishable stock, advance bookings, or events. A pub in Leeds that loses its beer garden for the entire summer after a storm does not just lose the repair cost. It loses the highest-margin quarter of the year.

What Does Business Interruption Insurance Cover?

Policies vary by insurer — AXA, Aviva, Hiscox, Direct Line Business, Simply Business — but the core structure under CII guidance is similar. You select a sum insured and an indemnity period. The indemnity period is not the policy length. It is the maximum time the insurer will pay loss of profit after the damage, starting from the date of damage.

What's Included — Limits Box

Limits based on standard UK SME wordings — check your schedule. Indemnity limits apply per occurrence unless stated.

Gross Profit / Gross Earnings

Turnover minus uninsured variable costs. Typically £50,000 – £750,000 sum insured for small shops; higher for restaurants and pubs. Covers shortfall versus expected trading.

Basis: Declared gross profit basis, assessed via accountant’s figures and Companies House.

Increased Cost of Working (ICOW)

Extra spend to keep trading: temporary premises, overtime, equipment hire, delivery fees. Common limit £10k-£50k for SMEs; often included within gross profit limit or as additional spend.

Example: Hiring a pop-up kitchen in Leeds to maintain delivery during refit.

Fixed Charges & Rent

Rent, business rates, loan interest, staff salaries under contract. May be included in gross profit definition or as separate standing charges. Indemnity period typically 12, 18 or 24 months.

FCA test case highlighted need to read rent payable extensions carefully.

Optional Coverages & Extensions

Standard UK BI requires material damage at your premises. Extensions widen the trigger, usually for extra premium. Not all insurers offer all extensions.

  • Suppliers and customers extension: Covers interruption due to damage at a named supplier or major customer — vital for retailers dependent on one wholesaler.
  • Prevention of access / non-damage: Loss because police, local authority, or HSE close roads or area after an incident nearby, even if your premises undamaged.
  • Utilities failure: Interruption due to failure of electricity, gas, water or telecoms from insured damage at the utility supplier — often limited to 500m-1km radius.
  • Loss of attraction: Reduced footfall because a nearby anchor store or attraction is damaged — common for shopping centre units in Newcastle or Manchester.
  • Book debts / outstanding debt: If accounting records destroyed, covers cost to reconstitute records and loss of uncollectable debts.
  • Disease, murder, closure: Limited disease cover for certain notifiable diseases at the premises, subject to FCA test case restrictions and aggregation limits. Usually sub-limited to £25k-£100k now, not full limit.

Always check if your policy uses a material damage proviso — meaning BI only pays if there is a valid property claim. That matters. If your property insurer declines fire damage for non-disclosure, your BI will also not respond.

What Is NOT Covered by Business Interruption Insurance UK? — Common Mistakes

Many declined claims happen because buyers assume BI covers any loss of income. It does not. The ABI and FCA both highlight these gaps.

⚠️ DANGER — 6 Exclusions & Costly Mistakes That Void Cover

  1. Non-damage interruption without extension: Pandemic lockdowns, staff shortages, or cyber-attacks with no physical damage at insured premises are not covered unless specific extension is bought and still in-force post-2021. The FCA Supreme Court judgment in 2021 narrowed non-damage BI drastically.
  2. Under-insurance & average clause: If you declare £200k gross profit but actual is £350k, many wordings apply average and pay only 57% of loss. CII warns this is the most expensive SME mistake — review sums insured quarterly against management accounts.
  3. Too-short indemnity period: Choosing 12 months to save premium when rebuild + fit-out + restocking + re-obtaining licence in London or Edinburgh takes 18-24 months. You run out of cover while still not back to expected profit.
  4. Wear and tear, gradual deterioration, mechanical breakdown: Standard property perils exclude these, so BI does not trigger. A coffee machine failing in a Glasgow café is not insured interruption unless you bought equipment breakdown extension.
  5. Voluntary closure or loss of licence due to own conduct: If Health and Safety Executive or local licensing authority closes you due to your own breaches of food safety or fire safety, BI will not pay. Compliance matters — HSE guidance requires risk assessments.
  6. Uninsured working expenses deducted incorrectly: If your policy is on gross profit basis and you misclassify which costs are variable vs standing charges, you under-declare insured standing charges and under-recover. HMRC payroll figures often help prove fixed staff costs.

How Does Business Interruption Insurance Work in the UK? — Claim Process

When fire, flood, or storm damages your trading premises, two claims run in parallel: material damage to repair, and BI to replace income. You must notify both claims quickly. The process is formal and accountant-heavy.

✅ SUCCESS — 6 Steps to Get Your Business Interruption Claim Paid Correctly

  1. Notify immediately & mitigate loss — Day 0-1: Call your insurer/broker and your property insurer. Make premises safe, photograph damage, preserve stock. You have a duty under Insurance Act 2015 to prevent further loss. Keep receipts for boarding up and emergency hire.
  2. Provide financial baseline — Day 2-7: Submit last 2-3 years accounts filed at Companies House, management accounts, gross profit calculation, VAT returns. Your accountant confirms expected turnover had loss not occurred.
  3. Agree indemnity period and measure — Ongoing: Insurer appoints loss adjuster. You track actual turnover vs expected, daily. ICOW spend (e.g., temporary kitchen, delivery fees) must be reasonable and aim to save turnover loss — save invoices.
  4. Document increased cost of working: Log extra staff overtime, relocation rent, equipment hire, marketing to tell customers you moved. Only costs additional to normal and incurred to maintain turnover qualify.
  5. Submit interim figures — Monthly: Most SME BI claims allow interim payments once liability agreed. Provide monthly profit & loss showing shortfall. HMRC payroll and bank statements support fixed salary element.
  6. Final adjustment and settlement: After indemnity period ends or you return to pre-loss trend, adjuster applies savings (e.g., reduced electricity when closed) and under-insurance check. Settlement = (Gross profit shortfall + ICOW) – Savings – Excess, capped at sum insured.

⚠️ WARNING — Common Delay That Reduces Payment

Business owners in Manchester and Birmingham often wait weeks to produce accounts, thinking BI is automatic. Insurers cannot calculate loss without management accounts and VAT returns. If you have not filed up-to-date accounts with Companies House and HMRC, start now. Missing baseline figures is the single biggest reason interim payments are delayed beyond 45 days.

Factors Increasing and Decreasing Your Business Interruption Cost

Increases cost:

  • Higher gross profit sum insured: Declaring £400k vs £150k directly scales premium; under-declaring risks average clause reduction.
  • Longer indemnity period: 24-month costs ~25-40% more than 12-month but essential for pubs requiring brewery refit and licence reissue.
  • High-risk trade or location: Takeaway with deep-fat fryers, pub in flood zone 3 in York, or restaurant on high street with previous escapes of water.
  • Dependency extensions: Adding suppliers extension, utilities (500m radius), and prevention of access each adds premium.
  • Claims history: Previous BI claims in last 5 years flagged by CII and ABI data sharing increase rating.

Decreases cost:

  • Accurate variable costs deduction: Correctly deducting uninsured working expenses like raw material purchases at 60% of turnover reduces gross profit basis legally.
  • Higher voluntary excess: Moving from £100 to £500 excess can reduce illustrative premium by 5-12% per observed broker quotes — not guaranteed.
  • Risk management: HSE-recognised business continuity plan, fire suppression, water leak detection — evidence reduces both property and BI rating.
  • Bundling with commercial-property-insurance: Many insurers include £10k ICOW free when you buy combined buildings + BI package.
  • Better bookkeeping: Clean Companies House accounts and HMRC VAT returns reduce underwriter uncertainty and average clause risk, improving terms.

Commercial vs Personal: Why Business Cover Is Different

Personal home or contents insurance never covers business income. If you run a shop-insurance or restaurant-insurance risk from your home address, you still need commercial cover. A personal policy excludes loss of gross profit, fixed business expenses, and alternative trading costs. The ABI is clear that personal cover is for domestic perils, not trade.

Commercial cover is underwritten on gross profit, indemnity period, and material damage proviso. It expects you to keep audited financials, have an HSE business continuity assessment, and comply with Insurance Act 2015 fair presentation. For example, requesting a commercial property insurance quote that includes BI lets underwriters assess rebuild value and profit at risk together, often reducing administrative fees compared with separate policies. This does not guarantee a lower CPC or lower premium; pricing depends on individual risk and insurer appetite at quote time.

Bottom line: If customers pay you, you are commercial. Buying personal-level cover leaves your rent, payroll, and loan exposure completely uninsured during a closure.

How to Save Money on Business Interruption Insurance UK — 6 Practical Tips

  1. Choose the right basis — gross profit vs gross earnings vs gross fees: Cafés and shops on 55-70% variable cost basis benefit from gross profit (turnover minus purchases). Consultants should use fees basis. Getting basis right avoids over-insuring by £50k-£100k sum insured, which directly affects premium.
  2. Match indemnity period to realistic recovery: Do not default to 12 months. A leasehold pub refit after fire in Newcastle typically takes 9-14 months excluding licensing. A 24-month period costs more but prevents the most severe gap — running out of cover in month 15 when still closed. Ask builder for realistic timeline.
  3. Document your business continuity plan: Insurers like Hiscox and Aviva reduce perceived risk if you provide an HSE-aligned plan: alternative suppliers list, remote work capability, temporary premises agreement. Some broker submissions show 5-10% illustrative discount for documented plans — not guaranteed, but helps negotiation.
  4. Increase excess and pay annually: Observed SME quotes in May 2026 show annual direct debit costing less than monthly instalments (which add finance charges ~8-15% APR). Raising voluntary excess from £250 to £500 can lower premium but only if you can absorb it.
  5. Combine covers under one commercial-insurance package: Buying shop-insurance that bundles public liability (£2m), property (£150k contents), and BI (£200k gross profit, 12 months) from one insurer reduces fees versus three separate policies. Simply Business panel data shows combined packs from £180-£350 illustrative for low-risk retail.
  6. Review sums insured quarterly — avoid average: Before renewal, compare gross profit declared vs actual turnover from HMRC self-assessment and Companies House filings. If turnover grew 20% after adding delivery revenue, increase sum insured. This avoids the dangerous average clause that can cut a claim in half even though you paid premium all year.

FAQ: Business Interruption Insurance UK

Is business interruption insurance required by law in the UK?

No. Unlike employers’ liability insurance, BI is not required by statute. However, commercial landlords, lenders, and many franchise agreements require at least 12-24 months BI. The FCA expects you to consider it as part of suitable advice for trading businesses with fixed costs.

How long does the indemnity period need to be for a pub or restaurant?

Most insurance brokers in the UK recommend 18-24 months for pubs and restaurants. Rebuild after fire, plus refit, environmental health inspections, licensing, and staff rehiring often exceeds 12 months. Choosing too short a period leaves you without income while still closed. Check your lease — many require 24 months.

Does business interruption insurance cover pandemics or lockdowns?

Standard UK BI wordings since 2021 generally do not cover pandemics unless you have a specific disease extension that meets FCA test case wording, usually sub-limited to £25,000-£100,000. Most small-business-insurance packages now exclude or severely restrict non-damage business interruption. Always read the extension wording, not just the marketing summary.

What is the difference between gross profit and gross revenue for BI?

Gross revenue is turnover. Gross profit for BI in the UK is turnover minus uninsured variable costs (e.g., raw materials, wholesale purchases). The Chartered Insurance Institute recommends using accounting definitions verified by your accountant. Using revenue over-insures and costs more premium unnecessarily.

Can I claim BI if my supplier has a fire, not my premises?

Only if you purchased suppliers extension and the supplier is named or within territory limits. Standard BI requires insured damage at your own premises. With suppliers extension, damage at a named supplier that stops your stock can trigger cover, subject to inner limits and excess. Without it, claim will be declined.

How does the average clause work on business interruption?

If you insure for £200,000 gross profit but actual gross profit is £300,000, you are under-insured by one-third. Insurer may reduce claim payment by same proportion. For example, a £90,000 loss becomes £60,000 payable. This is why accurate HMRC and Companies House data matters. The ABI highlights under-insurance as a major cause of complaints.
