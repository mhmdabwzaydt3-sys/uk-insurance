Employers' Liability vs Public Liability Insurance UK 2026: Coverage, Costs & Legal Requirements (2026 Guide)

If you employ anyone in the UK and meet clients or work on third-party premises, you need to understand Employers Liability vs Public Liability insurance fast. This guide explains what is legally compulsory, what each covers, what it costs in 2026, and how to buy them together without leaving a gap that could cost you thousands. Includes exact limits, real-world claim steps, and money-saving tips.

Quick Answer For Featured Snippet

Employers liability insurance is legally required in the UK if you have staff, with a minimum £5m cover for employee injury or illness. Public liability is not legally required but covers injury or property damage to members of the public. Most businesses with employees need both; average combined cost in 2026 ranges £110-£450/year for low-risk SMEs.

What is Employers Liability vs Public Liability Insurance?

Think of liability insurance as two shields. You need the right shield for the right person. If a customer slips, that’s public. If your own apprentice trips over cables in your workshop, that’s employers.

Employers Liability (EL) is about your duty of care to people you employ. The Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 says you must cover them for bodily injury or disease caused by the job. It includes labour-only subcontractors in most cases. Public Liability (PL) is about your duty to everyone else — clients, suppliers, delivery drivers, passers-by. It pays compensation, NHS charges, and legal defence if you’re liable for their injury or for damaging their property.

Are they the same business? Not at all. You can be sued by an employee and by a member of public on the same project, but each claim goes to a different section of your policy. That’s why insurers sell a combined package.

Who Needs It? Why Is It Required in the UK?

If you are asking whether you can skip one, pause. HSE enforcement is active in 2026, and many clients won’t let you on site without proof of both.

Checklist: Do You Need Employers, Public, or Both?

  • ✓You employ staff, even part-time or temporary: You legally need employers liability. Includes trainees, apprentices, labour-only subs in most cases.
  • ✓You work at client premises: You need public liability. One burst pipe or scratched floor can cost £5k-£30k.
  • ✓You have visitors to your premises (shop, salon, studio, warehouse): Public liability essential for slip, trip, and stock damage claims.
  • ✓You are a limited company with only a director: If you have more than one director and no other staff, you generally still need EL if you have any other employees; if you are sole owner with no staff, EL may be exempt – check with insurer.
  • ✓You use bona fide subcontractors: They should have own insurance, but you still need PL to cover their impact on public. If they are labour-only, they count as employees for EL.
  • ✓You tender for council, retail, or construction contracts: Most require EL £10m and PL £2m-£5m minimum. Without both, you fail pre-qualification.
  • ✓Family members working for you: Yes, unless you are a family business with all employees closely related and registered as such – exemption is narrow. When unsure, you must have EL.

EL must be from FCA-authorised insurer and certificate must show insurer name, policy number, start/end dates, and minimum £5m limit per HSE rules.

There is also a legal nuance many miss. Your EL certificate must be accessible to all employees. Under HSE rules, you must display it where staff can see it, or make it available electronically to all. If you have staff working across multiple sites – say a shop in Leeds, a warehouse in Bristol, and installers covering Glasgow – electronic access with clear notification satisfies the rule. Failing to display can trigger a £1,000 fine even if you are insured. Public liability has no such display rule, but you will be asked to email your certificate to clients.

Another practical difference is who pays first. With PL, you usually have an excess – often £100 to £500 – that you pay on every property damage claim. With EL, many insurers offer £0 excess for injury, but may apply excess for property damage (employees’ possessions). Always check your schedule. This matters for budgeting. If you have high staff turnover, your payroll figure changes, and mid-term adjustment fees may apply.

Optional Coverages to Consider

Basic combined liability does not cover everything. Depending on turnover, payroll, claims postcode, crime rate, and flood exposure, consider:

  • Product liability: If you supply goods, even if you don’t manufacture, you can be liable for injury caused by them.
  • Contract works / all risks: Covers works in progress, materials, scaffolding, tools on site – critical for builders and fit-out firms.
  • Professional indemnity: For design, advice, or specification errors – e.g., mis-specifying load capacity.
  • Business vehicle & commercial auto: If you drive vans with stock to site, personal car insurance won’t cover goods in transit. You must declare business use.

What is NOT Covered? Common Mistakes That Leave You Exposed

Danger Box Strict Exclusions – Gap competitors miss
  • 1. Motor-related liability: Both EL and PL exclude injury caused by motor vehicles that should be insured under Road Traffic Act. You need motor or commercial auto insurance quote for vans and cars.
  • 2. Deliberate acts or known defects: If you knew scaffolding was faulty and kept using it, insurers can void cover for working at height or structural collapse claims.
  • 3. Labour-only vs bona fide subcontractor confusion: Mistaking labour-only subs for bona fide subcontractors means your EL gap is uninsured and HSE can fine you. Check CIS status and control test.
  • 4. Employee-to-employee property damage: Standard EL covers injury, not your staff’s personal property. Tools cover needed separately.
  • 5. Gradual pollution, wear and tear, subsidence: Public liability typically excludes gradual damage – only sudden, accidental events. Flood-prone postcodes may need separate flood cover.
  • 6. Off-shore, height, and heat work without declaration: If you do work above 10m, with heat, or at depth but didn’t declare, PL can be declined. Always declare work at height, scaffolding use, and use of power tools.

How Does a Claim Work? Step-by-Step Process for Both Covers

Success Box Correct Order – How to File a Claim
  1. Make safe & document: First aid, photos of scene, witness names, CCTV. For an employee injury, log in accident book per HSE.
  2. Notify insurer immediately: Most policies require notification within 24-48 hours for injury, 7 days for property damage. Don’t admit liability.
  3. Identify correct section: Is claimant staff (EL) or public (PL)? This determines which limit, excess, and notification form applies.
  4. Provide documents: EL certificate, risk assessments, training records, method statements, payroll proof. For PL: contract, photos, maintenance logs.
  5. Insurer appoints loss adjuster / solicitor: They handle negotiation. You must cooperate, attend court if needed, and keep records for 40+ years for EL disease claims.
  6. Settlement & lessons learned: Compensation paid from limit; excess deducted. Update risk assessment to avoid future premium hike from claims history.

Warning Box Common Mistake – Mixing Up Subcontractors

I see this mistake daily. A business owner says “they’re self-employed, so I don’t need EL”. HSE looks at control, not what you call them. If you provide tools, set hours, and they can’t send a substitute, they are likely labour-only and count as employees. That means you must have EL, payroll records, and Employers’ Liability Tracing Office (ELTO) data submitted. Check your insurer’s definition before you hire anyone in Manchester, London or Birmingham – postcodes with higher crime or flood risk already increase your premium, but mis-declaration can void cover entirely.

Commercial vs Personal / Basic vs Pro: Why Free or Personal Cover Fails Businesses

Commercial Box Profit Secret for AdSense Relevance

A home insurance public liability extension or a free “personal accident” bolt-on does NOT meet the Employers’ Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 and will not satisfy a council PQQ. Personal policies exclude work, staff, and work-related property damage. They also have no Employers’ Liability Tracing Office (ELTO) reporting, so your EL certificate would be invalid.

If you use your own car to transport tools or travel between sites, personal car insurance is insufficient. You need to declare business use, and in many cases you need a business vehicle insurance policy or commercial auto insurance quote for vans under business use class. This is the same principle: commercial risk needs commercial wording, not a domestic free add-on. Insurers will check business use at claim stage, and a personal policy can be voided for non-disclosure, leaving both EL and PL gaps. Similarly, business interruption, loss of rent, building damage, and stock theft for a shop/retail business are not covered under personal lines – you need commercial cover that accounts for turnover, payroll, postcode crime, and flood risk.

Not all insurers offer identical wording. Check your policy schedule for ELTO and FCA authorised status. Cost varies by insurer, turnover and payroll; see limits box above.

FAQ: Employers' Liability vs Public Liability Insurance UK

What is the main difference between employers liability and public liability insurance? ⌄

Public liability protects your business when a member of the public is injured or their property damaged because of your business. Employers liability protects you when your own employee is injured or becomes ill due to work. One covers third parties, the other covers staff. You often need both.

Is employers liability insurance a legal requirement in the UK? ⌄

Yes. Under the Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969, almost all UK employers with staff must have at least £5 million of employers liability cover from an authorised insurer, display the certificate, and show it to HSE inspectors on request. Fines can reach £2,500 per day plus £1,000 for not displaying.

Do I need public liability if I already have employers liability? ⌄

Often yes. EL does not cover public claims. If clients visit your premises, you work in their home, or you work in public spaces, public liability is essential. Many contracts, local councils and trade bodies require proof of £1m to £5m public liability before you can tender or work on site.

How much employers liability cover do I need by law? ⌄

UK law requires a minimum limit of indemnity of £5 million. In practice, most insurers automatically provide £10 million as standard to meet contract requirements. The limit applies per occurrence and includes legal costs. Check your policy schedule for the exact limit.

Can I buy employers and public liability together? ⌄

Yes. Most UK business insurers offer a combined liability package that includes both employers and public liability, often with product liability included. Buying together is usually cheaper than separate policies, simplifies certificates, and ensures no gap between staff and public claims.

What happens if I don't have employers liability insurance when I employ staff? ⌄

The Health and Safety Executive can fine you £2,500 for every day you are uninsured and £1,000 for failing to display your EL certificate or produce it on inspection. You would also be personally liable for compensation, legal fees and NHS costs if an employee is injured, which can reach six or seven figures.
