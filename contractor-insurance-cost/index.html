What is Contractor Insurance Cost UK?

Contractor insurance is not a single policy. It is a bundled liability and property package built for self-employed trades and limited-company contractors who work on client sites. You need it when your work could injure a third party, damage property, or cause financial loss because of faulty advice or workmanship.

Unlike a basic public liability slip for a market trader, contractor insurance accounts for working at height, hot works, tools left in vans overnight, and contract works in progress. Short sentence. It reflects the higher severity of builder and roofing claims, where one scaffold collapse can exceed £100,000, so insurers price trade, height, and heat exposure differently. Do you know your policy limit is per claim, not per year for some covers?

Who Needs Contractor Insurance? Why Is It Required?

You must consider contractor insurance if you work on domestic or commercial sites without being a PAYE employee. Not all clients will ask. Most principal contractors will.

✅ Checklist: You Likely Need It If...
  • ✔️You are self-employed builder, roofer, carpenter, scaffolder, bricklayer, groundworker or shopfitter working in Sheffield, Newcastle, Bristol or any UK site
  • ✔️You employ labourers, apprentices or subcontractors under your direction - Employers' Liability is legally required under Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969, minimum £5m, market offers £10m
  • ✔️Your contract or CSCS site access requires public liability £2m-£5m and proof of contract works insurance
  • ✔️You work at height above 3m, use scaffolding, ladders, or roof ladders - standard policies exclude this
  • ✔️You have own tools worth £1,500+ or leave tools in a van overnight - tool cover is not in home insurance
  • ✔️You give design, supervision or advice (e.g., measurements, structural suggestions) - you may need professional indemnity element
  • ✔️You are a limited company contractor with turnover above £50k and JCT contract requirements for all-risk contract works

Legal note: Public liability is not a criminal law requirement in the UK, but employers' liability is. Check your contract wording. Acknowledging limits: not all insurers cover heat work, piling, or demolition - check policy wording.

What Does It Cover?

A typical UK contractor package is built around builder-specific risks: working at height, structural collapse, scaffolding strike, and material damage before handover. You pay for defence costs and compensation.

What's Included - Typical Limits (UK, 2026)

Public Liability
  • Limit: £1m, £2m or £5m per claim - VARIABLE by contract. £2m most common for domestic builders in England.
  • Covers injury to public or client, damage to property e.g., scaffold falling onto car, debris cracking patio.
  • Excess: £250-£500 typical
Employers' Liability
  • Legally required if you employ staff. Minimum £5m by law, market standard £10m (VERIFIED: HSE guidance on Employers' Liability Act 1969).

Tools, Plant & Contract Works
  • Tools: £2,000-£10,000 sum insured, often single item limit £1,500.
  • Contract works: covers works in progress - fire, theft, storm, vandalism up to contract value, e.g., £100k-£500k.
  • Own plant/hired-in plant £10k-£50k optional

Optional Coverages - Add When Relevant

You must match optional covers to trade risks. Not all insurers cover every risk, and cost varies by inclusion.

  • Contractors All Risks / Contract Works: Essential if you build extensions, roofs, timber frames. Covers materials on site, theft, flood. Example: £150k new extension in Sheffield - storm blows over unroofed section.
  • Tools & Equipment: For builders: power tools, laser levels, nail guns. Check overnight van exclusion: many require tools in locked van with alarm, or removed to locked building between 21:00-06:00.
  • Hired-in Plant: If you hire diggers, dumpers, cherry pickers - covers your liability for loss/damage to hired item.
  • Personal Accident & Income Protection: Pays £100-£400/week if you cannot work after fall from scaffolding.
  • Professional Indemnity for Design & Build: For contractors who draw plans, specify steel, advise on load. Without it, a negligent design exclusion applies to PL.
  • Public Liability Height Extension: Standard up to 10m. Roofers above 15m need specialist. Always declare max working height.

What is NOT Covered / Strictly Excluded / Common Mistakes

Builder policies contain strict exclusions. Gap competitors miss: height, heat, underground services.

⛔ Danger Box: 6 Exclusions That Void Builder Claims
  1. Working above declared height: If you declared 10m but work at 12m on a three-storey scaffold in Bristol, PL claim for fallen slate injuring pedestrian may be declined. Height is VARIABLE and warranty in wording.
  2. Heat work without permit: Use of blow torch, bitumen boiler, angle grinder producing sparks within 1 hour without fire watch - many AXA/Aviva wordings exclude unless you have hot works clause and precautions logged.
  3. Underground services strike: Hitting gas main or fibre without CAT scan records and utility searches - exclusion applies. You must show you made enquiries.
  4. Faulty workmanship itself (not resulting damage): If your new flat roof leaks due to poor felt laying, policy does NOT pay to redo your faulty work. It may pay resulting damage to client's ceiling (depends on contractor extension). Cost varies by wording - check your policy wording.
  5. Tools left in van overnight without forced entry evidence: Insurers require evidence of forcible violent entry, van alarm, and tools hidden out of sight. "I left side door unlocked" = declined.
  6. Employers' liability for bona fide subcontractors treated as labour-only: If you engage CIS self-employed but control their hours/tools, they are deemed employees. Without EL, HSE fine and claim falls on you. This is a common mistake noted by HSE guidance.

How Does It Work? Step-by-Step

You buy contractor insurance through broker, insurer direct, or comparison. You pay monthly or annually. The insurer issues employers' liability certificate as VERIFIED legal document.

✅ Success Box: 6 Steps To Get Covered Without Gaps
  1. Define trade and activities: List exact activities: e.g., new build up to 2 storeys, loft conversions, roofing, scaffolding erection (if any), groundwork depth. Every number classified as VARIABLE - declare truthfully.
  2. Calculate sums needed: Public liability limit required by contract (ask principal contractor), turnover for last and next 12 months, wage roll, max contract value (£150k?), tool value (£5k?).
  3. Disclose height, heat, depth: State maximum working height (e.g., 8m for Newcastle two-storey semi), will you use heat? Will you dig below 1m? This changes price from £15/month to £40+/month.
  4. Get quotes with same assumptions: Use identical turnover £85k, PL £2m, EL £10m, tools £5k, contract works £150k across all providers. Otherwise comparison is meaningless.
  5. Read warranty - don't skip: Search PDF for "warranty", "condition precedent", "hot works", "height". If you see "You must..." it is mandatory, not advisory.
  6. Issue certificates to site: Provide EL certificate (required by law to display), PL schedule, contract works proof to main contractor. Keep digital copy in van and office.

Factors Affecting Contractor Insurance Cost

You pay more when severity rises. Insurers load for roofing vs carpentry, for Leeds city-centre vs rural, for £200k turnover vs £40k. Not all insurers cover same height.

  • Trade code & risk: Roofer working at 12m with torch-on felt = highest loading. Carpenter second fix at ground = lower. Electrical and Gas Safe work adds PI loading. Builder doing structural alterations = higher than painting. Cost varies by declared activities - check your policy wording to see excluded activities list.
  • Working at height & depth: Standard policies allow 8-10m. Working above 10m can add 25-60% premium. Working above 15m needs specialist broker. Depth below 1.5m (foundations) may require underpinning exclusion removed.
  • Turnover, wage roll & contract value: £80k turnover with one employee vs £200k with three employees = EL increase £180-£400/year. Contract works sum £100k vs £500k = all-risk price doubles. Declare estimate accurately - under-insurance leads to average clause.
  • Claims history (3-5 years): Zero claims last 5 years = up to 20% discount with some insurers. One public liability claim £8k in last 2 years can increase renewal 35-70%. Some insurers decline if two PL claims.
  • Location & where you work: Working in Central London on commercial sites with £5m limit required costs more than domestic South Yorkshire. Postcode rating applies to tool theft: Newcastle NE1 van theft higher than rural Durham.
  • Qualifications & safety: Having SMSTS, SSSTS, CSCS Black Card, NICEIC, Gas Safe, City & Guilds, or membership in FMB (Federation of Master Builders) can reduce premium 5-15% with certain brokers. No qualifications plus heat work = many declines.

Commercial vs Personal: Why Your Home Insurance Won't Cover Site Work

Personal liability or domestic home insurance does NOT cover you for paid building work. Home insurance excludes business activity for reward. If you damage client property while being paid, home insurer declines.

You may see search terms like business vehicle insurance or requests for a commercial auto insurance quote when you also need to insure your transit van for carrying tools and materials. Those are separate covers. Standard car insurance or social domestic pleasure does not cover carriage of own tools for hire and reward, nor carriage of waste. You need carriage of own goods or tools, plus business use class. Using personal car policy to carry scaffolding to site = uninsured third-party driving - separate offence under Road Traffic Act 1988 (VERIFIED: gov.uk vehicle insurance requirements). Cost varies by van, no-claims, and whether you have tipper or sign-written van.

Similarly, a landlord building insurance quote is for property owner risk, not contractor liability for building it. Landlord covers buildings, loss of rent, subsidence. Contractor covers liability while constructing. Don't confuse them - you need contractor cover if you build, landlord cover if you own and let.

How to Get Cheapest Contractor Insurance UK

You can reduce contractor insurance cost UK without cutting essential limits. You must keep legally required EL £10m and contract-compliant PL. Saving comes from accuracy, bundling and risk control.

  1. Declare accurate height and drop heat if not needed: If you never use torch, declare "No heat work". Removing heat can save £120-£280/year illustrative (VARIABLE). If max height is 6m but you declared 15m as worst case, reducing to 8m can save £90-£200/year.
  2. Bundle PL+EL+tools+contract works with one insurer: Buying separate policies costs more admin and duplicate fees. Bundled contractor package via Simply Business panel often £19-£49/month vs £34-£70 buying separate. Illustrative range observed Aug 2026, varies.
  3. Increase excess strategically: Raising PL excess from £250 to £500 can cut 8-12% premium. Example: £38/month → £34/month (CALCULATED HYPOTHETICAL saving £48/year). Only do if you can afford excess.
  4. Pay annually, not monthly: Monthly instalments add 6-15% APR. Example: £420 annual vs 12 x £38.50 = £462 (HYPOTHETICAL CALCULATION: £42 extra). If you have cash flow, annual saves.
  5. Tool security measures: Van alarm + tool safe + removing tools overnight to locked premises reduces tool premium from £120/year to £70/year with some insurers (VARIABLE). Provide photos/receipts for tools over £500 to avoid underinsurance.
  6. Zero claims + CPD proof: Keep CSCS card, SMSTS certificate, method statements, RAMS. Some Hiscox/AXA schemes offer 5-10% renewal loyalty if no claims and evidence of CPD. Example: Professional Indemnity for design & build drops from £350 to £315/year with CPD log (HYPOTHETICAL VARIABLE).

FAQ – Contractor Insurance Cost UK

How much does contractor insurance cost per month in the UK?

For a self-employed builder, illustrative range observed 15 Aug 2026 is £6.20-£29/month for public liability £1m-£2m only, and £19.50-£68/month for a full package with employers’ liability £10m, tools £5k and contract works £100k. Cost is VARIABLE by trade, turnover, height, postcode and claims history - get official quote.

Do I legally need contractor insurance in UK?

Employers’ liability is legally required under Employers’ Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 if you employ staff. Minimum legal limit is £5m, market gives £10m. Public liability is not a legal requirement, but most contracts and principal contractors require £2m-£5m before site access under CDM Regulations 2015 (HSE).

What is the cheapest contractor insurance for self-employed builders?

Cheapest is usually PL £1m only via broker panel like Simply Business from £6.48/month illustrative (observed Aug 2026). But £1m is often insufficient for commercial sites needing £5m. Check your contract - buying cheapest then upgrading later costs mid-term fees. Compare like-for-like limits.

Does contractor insurance cover tools and scaffolding?

Tools up to £10k can be added, with single item limits. Scaffolding you own can be covered as business equipment; hired scaffolding needs hired-in plant cover. Overnight van storage requires forcible entry evidence and alarm. Own scaffolding collapse causing injury is under PL, but scaffolding erection itself may need specialist extension.

Can I get contractor insurance without employers’ liability?

Yes, if you have no employees and use bona fide subcontractors who have own insurance. If you use labour-only subcontractors you control, they count as employees and you legally need EL. Fines for no EL are up to £2,500 per day per HSE guidance. Always check subcontractor status carefully.

What affects contractor insurance cost UK most?

Top factors: trade risk (roofer highest), max working height, use of heat, turnover, wage roll, contract works value, claims history, and postcode tool theft risk. Qualifications like SMSTS, CSCS, FMB membership and robust RAMS can reduce premium 5-15% with some insurers. Cost varies by insurer - check policy wording.
