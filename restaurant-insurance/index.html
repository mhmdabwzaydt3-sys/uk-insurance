Restaurant Insurance UK 2026: Coverage, Costs, Requirements & Claims (2026 Guide)

Restaurant Insurance UK protects your restaurant, staff, customers and income when something goes wrong. If you run a cafe, bistro, takeaway or full-service restaurant, you need the right mix of public liability, employers' liability, property and business interruption. This guide shows you exactly what is required by law, what to include, what it costs in 2026, and how to claim without losing money.

Quick Answer • Featured Snippet

Restaurant Insurance UK is a bundled business policy for restaurants that must include public liability and employers' liability if you have staff. It typically covers customer injury, property damage, stock, equipment, and loss of income. Costs vary by turnover, covers and claims history.

What is Restaurant Insurance UK?

Restaurant insurance isn't one single policy. It's a package. You buy core liability covers and bolt on property, stock, equipment and interruption covers so your venue is protected in one place. Unlike a standard shop policy, it knows your risks are different. Open kitchens, hot oil, alcohol service, late hours, delivery riders, high footfall. One slip near the pass and you face a claim.

Think of it this way. Personal home insurance covers your sofa at home. A regular business policy might cover a quiet office. Restaurant Insurance UK covers a busy kitchen with gas appliances, sharp tools, food safety risks, and dozens of customers a day. That's why insurers price it separately.

If you serve food to the public in the UK, insurers treat you as higher risk. You must prove you meet Food Standards, HSE safety rules, and FCA-regulated sales standards. Not all insurers offer restaurant cover, and many that do will ask for your food hygiene rating, extraction cleaning records, and alarm details.

Who Needs Restaurant Insurance in the UK? Why Is It Required?

If you prepare or serve food for money, you fall into this bucket. Landlords, local councils, and delivery platforms will ask for proof. The law also forces your hand if you employ anyone, even part-time.

Checklist – You likely need Restaurant Insurance UK if you:

  • Run a restaurant, bistro, gastropub, cafe, takeaway, fast-food shop, or cloud kitchen
  • Employ any staff, including family members, students, or seasonal workers – employers' liability is a legal requirement under UK law
  • Have customers on premises where slips, burns, or allergic reactions could happen
  • Own or lease kitchen equipment, refrigeration, tills, furniture, and fit-out worth thousands
  • Hold perishable stock – failing freezers can wipe out £3,000-£8,000 in one night
  • Rent a unit and your lease requires you to hold property and public liability insurance
  • Offer delivery, either with your own drivers or via Just Eat / Deliveroo / Uber Eats
  • Serve alcohol – your licensing conditions often require liability cover to stay valid

What Does Restaurant Insurance Cover? Requirements in the UK

A complete Restaurant Insurance UK package is built from compulsory and optional parts. You choose limits based on your size. Bigger turnover, more covers, late licence, and wood-fired grill? You pay more.

What's Included – Standard Covers with Typical Limits

Liability Covers

  • Public Liability: £2m, £5m or £10m – injury to customers / damage to their property
  • Products Liability: £2m-£5m – food poisoning, foreign object, allergic reaction
  • Employers' Liability: £10m as standard, legal minimum £5m – injury / illness to staff

Property & Stock

  • Buildings: rebuild value if you own – often £250k-£1.5m+
  • Contents & Equipment: £10k-£250k – ovens, fryers, fridges, POS
  • Stock & Perishables: £5k-£50k, plus deterioration after freezer failure £2k-£15k

Financial Protection

  • Business Interruption: 12-24 months indemnity, covers gross profit loss after insured damage
  • Loss of Licence: £5k-£50k if you lose alcohol licence for approved reasons
  • Money & Theft: £1k-£10k on premises, in transit to bank

Limits shown are typical market ranges seen in UK restaurant policies. Your actual limits depend on quote. Check your policy schedule.

Optional Coverages Worth Adding

  • Tenants Improvement: fit-out you paid for in a leased unit
  • Goods in Transit: stock being moved between sites or from suppliers
  • Legal Expenses: £50k-£100k for employment disputes, HMRC investigations, licence appeals
  • Personal Accident for directors: weekly benefit if you cannot work
  • Cyber & Till hack: for EPOS data breach and loss
  • Engineering & Equipment breakdown: boilers, extraction, walk-in chillers

What is NOT Covered? Common Exclusions and Mistakes

This is where restaurants get rejected. Many owners assume everything kitchen-related is covered. It's not. Policies exclude things you can control, neglect, or didn't tell the insurer about, and those gaps show up at claim time.

Danger – 6 Exclusions That Lead to Rejected Claims

  1. No cover for poor hygiene or lack of maintenance: If you haven't cleaned extraction to insurer-required frequency (often every 6-12 months with proof) or you have pest infestation without treatment records, fire or contamination claims can be denied.
  2. Wear, tear, and gradual deterioration: A 12-year-old fryer that finally fails isn't accidental damage. Breakdown cover is separate and requires servicing records.
  3. Deliberate deep-fat fryer misuse or unattended cooking: Many policies exclude fire caused by leaving frying appliances unattended or using domestic appliances commercially.
  4. Undisclosed refurbishment, change of use, or subletting: Adding a late-night bar area, extending to outside seating with heaters, or subletting to a ghost kitchen without notifying insurer voids cover.
  5. Employers working without Employers' Liability: If you have one part-time dishwasher and no EL policy, HSE can fine £2,500 per day you are uninsured, and you pay injury costs yourself. No insurer will backdate it.
  6. Business interruption without physical damage at premises: Standard BI needs insured property damage at your site. Closure just because footfall drops or supply chain fails without damage at your premises is usually not covered unless you buy specific extensions.

How Does Restaurant Insurance Work? How to Claim Step-by-Step

You buy a schedule. It lists what is insured, limits, excesses, and conditions. If an incident happens, you notify quickly and document everything. Slow reporting hurts you.

Success Path – 6 Steps to a Correct Restaurant Claim

  1. Make safe and prevent further loss: Isolate gas/electric if safe, call emergency services, move stock to working freezer. Don't throw away damaged goods until insurer says.
  2. Notify insurer/broker within 24 hours: Use claim line. Give policy number, date, cause, estimated loss. Late notification can reduce payout.
  3. Gather evidence in order: Photos of damage, extraction cleaning certificates, temperature logs, food hygiene records, CCTV of incident, supplier invoices for stock value.
  4. Keep full financial trail: Till reports, delivery receipts, payroll, recent accounts for business interruption calculations. You need gross profit proof.
  5. Do not admit liability to a customer or staff member: Pass any injury letters to insurer immediately. Saying “sorry, our fault” can be used against you.
  6. Get repair quotes from approved trades: 2-3 quotes for equipment, builders report for buildings. For BI, work with loss adjuster on mitigation – e.g., temporary takeaway-only trade.
  7. Track recovery and settle: Accept interim payment for stock if offered. Final settlement after forensic accounts for interruption often takes 30-90 days.

Illustrative Scenario – HYPOTHETICAL Example

HYPOTHETICAL – Liverpool: Imagine a 40-cover Italian restaurant on Bold Street, Liverpool. At 9:30 pm a walk-in freezer compressor fails overnight. The owner discovers £4,200 of seafood and meat at unsafe temperature. She has £15,000 stock sum insured, £7,500 deterioration of stock limit, £250 excess. She has temperature logs and an engineer report showing sudden breakdown.

Less excess £250 = £5,030 payable under policy, assuming within limits and with service records. If she had no deterioration extension, only £0-£1,000 might be payable. This is a hypothetical illustration only, not a real claim and not a price promise.

Warning – Common Claim Mistake

Do not continue trading with a known gas leak or faulty fryer while waiting for an engineer, hoping it holds. That can breach your duty of care and your policy condition to prevent further loss. For example, continuing to fry during a Friday rush after spotting a fault can lead to fire spread, and insurers can reduce payout by 40% for failing to mitigate. Check your policy wording and act safely, not commercially.

Factors That Increase or Decrease Your Price

  • Cooking method: Deep fat fryers, charcoal, wood-fired ovens increase fire risk → +15-35% vs cold prep only
  • Turnover and footfall: £500k turnover vs £150k turnover can double BI sum needed and liability exposure
  • Staff numbers and wage roll: Employers' liability premium tracks wage bill; 10 staff costs more than 2
  • Claims history: One public liability claim settled £8k last year can add 20-40% at renewal
  • Location and theft: City centre with late hours and higher crime → higher property excess
  • Security and risk management: Monitored alarm, fire suppression over cooking, 5-star food hygiene, documented extraction cleans every 6 months can reduce premium
  • Excess level: Choosing £500 excess instead of £250 can cut premium ~10-15%, but you pay more at claim

Commercial vs Personal / Basic vs Pro – Why Personal Cover Won't Protect Your Restaurant

Home or personal liability policies do not cover commercial cooking, serving paying customers, or employing staff. If you try to claim a restaurant fire under personal contents, it will be declined. Insurers split risk like this: personal = domestic, non-profit, occasional; commercial = any activity for money, with public access, regulated premises.

Basic or free-tier business cover often gives you £1m public liability and no deterioration or business interruption. That's not PRO enough for a restaurant that would lose £2,000-£5,000 per day closed. You need the proper commercial restaurant package.

When shopping, you’ll see comparison sites offering a restaurant business insurance quote – that is the commercial intent term you should search for to get hospitality-rated providers, not generic shop quotes. Getting a commercial quote does not guarantee any CPC or ranking; it just ensures the underwriter prices your deep fryers, alcohol service and stock correctly.

How to Get Cheapest Restaurant Insurance – 6 Practical Ways to Save

  1. Bundle all covers with one insurer: Instead of 3 separate policies, bundle public, employers' and property together. Many UK providers discount 10-20% for bundle vs standalone. Example: £75/month separate vs £60/month bundled = £180 saved yearly.
  2. Increase voluntary excess to £500 where cashflow allows: Moving from £100 to £500 excess can cut premium £8-£20/month for low-claims venues. Only do this if you have £500 emergency reserve.
  3. Prove risk management: Upload 5-star Food Hygiene rating, gas safety cert, PAT testing, and biannual extraction clean invoice. Insurers have cut £120-£300/year for documented controls.
  4. Adjust stock sum seasonally: Don't insure £40k stock year-round if you only hold £12k low season. Review quarterly. Reducing declared stock £40k to £20k low season can save ~£15/month.
  5. Pay annually, not monthly: Monthly instalments often include credit charges 8-15% APR. A £900 annual premium monthly might total £990-£1,035. Annual saves £90-£135.
  6. Limit delivery risk correctly: If you use own drivers, you need separate motor / delivery cover, but if you use only Deliveroo/Just Eat riders who are self-employed and insured by platform, you avoid own-driver liability add-on which can be £300-£800/year.

FAQ – Restaurant Insurance UK

Is restaurant insurance a legal requirement in the UK?

Not all parts, but one is. Employers' liability insurance is legally required if you employ anyone, even part-time or family, with a minimum £5m cover. Public liability is not law but required by landlords, councils and delivery platforms, and strongly advised. Without it you pay claims yourself.

How much does Restaurant Insurance UK cost per month?

For a small takeaway with basic liability it can be £18-£44/month, for a 40-seat restaurant with employers' liability for 3 staff, stock and 12-month business interruption about £35-£102/month. These are VARIABLE illustrative ranges observed 10 May 2026 on comparison sites, not guaranteed. Your postcode, cooking type and claims change price.

Does restaurant insurance cover food poisoning claims?

Yes, products liability within Restaurant Insurance UK covers illness or injury from food you supplied, including allergic reactions, provided you followed food safety law and can show hygiene procedures. You must have temperature, traceability and allergy records. Deliberate breach of Food Safety Act is excluded.

What is not covered by standard restaurant insurance?

Gradual wear, faulty workmanship, undocumented maintenance, unattended frying, undeclared changes, cyber loss of customer data unless you add cyber, and interruption without physical damage at premises. Freezer deterioration often needs a specific add-on – check your schedule.

Do I need public liability if I only do takeaway and delivery?

Yes, you still need public and products liability. A customer can be injured collecting food, or claim food poisoning after delivery. Platforms like Just Eat require you to hold £2m-£5m public liability. Without it, your lease and platform contract can be terminated.

Can I get restaurant insurance with a 2 or 3 food hygiene rating?

Some insurers will, but many charge more or impose conditions. A low rating signals higher risk. Improving to 4 or 5 and showing corrective actions, staff training certs and recent EHO correspondence can reduce premium and make more insurers quote you at renewal.

Does Restaurant Insurance UK cover loss of income after a fire?

If you have business interruption cover, yes – it pays gross profit lost while your premises is unusable due to insured damage like fire, flood or impact. You choose indemnity period, 12 months is minimum recommended, 24 months safer for major rebuilds. You need accounts to prove loss.
