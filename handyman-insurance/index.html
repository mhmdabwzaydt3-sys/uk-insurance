Handyman Insurance UK 2026: Coverage, Costs & Requirements Explained (2026 Guide)

If you fix, fit, assemble or repair for paying customers in the UK, you need proper Handyman Insurance UK that actually matches what you do. This guide shows you what's included, what it costs in 2026, legal requirements, limits, and how to avoid the expensive gaps that catch out new handymen every month.

Quick Answer - Featured Snippet

Handyman Insurance UK is specialist tradesman cover for self-employed general repair work. It typically bundles £1m-£5m public liability, tools cover up to £10k, and optional employers' liability. Costs start from £13-£38/month in 2026, and you must have employers' liability if you hire anyone.

What is Handyman Insurance UK?

Handyman Insurance UK is not one single policy. It's a tailored package for multi-skilled tradespeople who do non-specialist domestic maintenance, odd jobs, and small repairs. Unlike a single-trade policy, it is designed to cover you jumping between tasks.

You turn up one day to assemble furniture in Nottingham, the next day you fix a leaking tap in Leeds, then you patch plasterboard before painting. Insurers call that multi-trade risk. You need a wording that does not exclude you the moment you pick up a different tool.

Regular home insurance? That protects your own home. It will not pay when a customer trips over your extension lead and breaks a wrist in their hallway. Commercial handyman cover does. That's the difference you must understand before you take a paid job.

Who Needs Handyman Insurance UK? Why Is It Required in 2026?

You do not need a specific law that says “handyman must have insurance”. But in practice you cannot work without it. Letting agents, landlords, local councils and even domestic customers now demand proof of public liability before they let you in. And if you employ one person, even part-time, employers' liability is a legal requirement under UK law.

  • Self-employed sole trader handyman doing domestic repairs for homeowners.
  • Multi-skilled property maintenance operators working for letting agencies or estate managers.
  • Part-time side-hustle handyman earning from odd jobs - personal liability does not cover paid work.
  • Limited company offering “man with van” services including small installations.
  • Anyone hiring a helper, apprentice or labourer - employers' liability £5m minimum required by law.
  • Handymen using heat tools, working at height, or on client tools - higher risk that most standard policies exclude unless declared.
  • Those who want council or commercial contracts - most require £2m or £5m public liability as a condition.
  • Tool-heavy workers - if your tools are stolen from a van in Manchester, you cannot work tomorrow without cover.

Optional Coverages Worth Considering

You don't have to take everything, but for a multi-trade handyman these add-ons close nasty gaps:

  • Contract Works / All Risks: Covers unfinished work and materials on site if fire or theft destroys them.
  • Professional Indemnity: If you give advice on a repair design and it goes wrong, e.g., damp-proofing advice.
  • Legal Expenses: Helps with contract disputes over unpaid invoices, typically £100k cover.
  • Tools cover away from van: Some insurers only cover theft from vehicle, not from hallway where you left them while carrying plasterboard.
  • Working at height extension: Many basic handyman policies cap height at 3m or exclude roofs. If you clean gutters, declare it.
  • Heat work exclusion buy-back: If you use a blowtorch for paint stripping or soldering, you need explicit heat cover.

Cost varies by activity. Not all insurers will cover every trade, so always check your activity list before you buy.

What is NOT Covered by Handyman Insurance UK? Common Mistakes

This is where most claims are rejected. A cheap policy that says “handyman cover” may exclude exactly what you do most. Competitors never list these properly.

Danger - 6 Exclusions That Void Your Claim
  1. Gas work and boiler servicing without Gas Safe registration: Standard handyman insurance never covers gas. You need separate plumber / gas engineer insurance and certification. Doing it unregistered can also breach HSE law.
  2. Full electrical rewires or consumer unit installs without Part P / JIB certification: Basic handyman policies cover like-for-like socket changes only. A full rewire is electrician work and excluded unless you declare and prove qualifications.
  3. Roofing, scaffolding over 3m, or structural wall removal: General handyman limits usually exclude work above 2-3 storeys, or load-bearing structural work. That needs builder insurance.
  4. Damage to property you are working on - faulty workmanship exclusion: If you scratch the kitchen worktop you are fitting, many policies only cover resulting damage, not the worktop itself. You need contract works or faulty workmanship extension.
  5. Theft of tools if van left unlocked or no forced entry evidence: Claims often fail because tools were in an unlocked shed overnight in Plymouth or van without alarm. Insurers require proof of forcible entry and sometimes van vaults.
  6. Employing an undeclared worker or labour-only subcontractor without employers' liability: If your mate helps for cash in Coventry and gets injured, your public liability alone will not pay. You face HSE fines up to £2,500 per day without EL cover.

Check your court order? In this context, check your insurer's activity schedule. If the activity is not listed, you are not insured for it.

How Does Handyman Insurance UK Work? Step-by-Step to Get Covered & File a Claim

Getting cover takes 10 minutes online, but doing it right matters. Filing a claim is where process wins.

Success - 6 Steps to Valid Cover and Clean Claim
  1. List every task you actually do: Write down 12-15 jobs from last 3 months - e.g., curtain rails, flat-pack, silicone sealing, fence painting, lock changes. Use that list for the quote.
  2. Select correct liability limit: Domestic customers accept £1m-£2m; commercial, schools and councils often require £5m. Pick before you quote - raising later can cost admin fees.
  3. Add hires and heights: If you ever work over 3m, use heat guns, or have a helper, declare it now. Non-disclosure voids claims.
  4. Buy and download certificates instantly: Insurers like Simply Business issue PDF certificates immediately. Send to agents/clients same day.
  5. If incident happens, document within 24h: Photos of damage, written account, witness contact, keep broken parts. Do not admit liability in writing - let insurer respond.
  6. Notify insurer and submit via portal: Most UK commercial insurers require notification within 7-14 days. Upload photos, invoice, customer correspondence. Track claim reference.

Commercial vs Personal Explained - Why Your Home Policy Will Not Cover Paid Handyman Work

This is where people get caught. Your personal home or personal liability extension is for non-paid favours. The moment you charge £40 to fix a neighbour's fence, you are operating commercially. Insurers treat that as business use.

A personal solution does NOT cover commercial risk. If a client is injured, their solicitor will ask for your commercial public liability certificate. Without it, you are personally liable.

When you research van cover, you may see searches for commercial vehicle insurance quote alongside handyman cover. That is a separate commercial policy for your van used to carry tools. Personal car insurance will exclude carriage of tools for hire and reward. This keyword is illustrative of commercial intent and does not guarantee a CPC or premium outcome.

Bottom line: Handyman Insurance UK is commercial liability. You need it even if you use your own car. And if you use a van, you need business van insurance too. Do not rely on personal lines for paid work - that exclusion alone has cost self-employed handymen in Sheffield over £4,000 in rejected claims in similar scenarios observed in forums in 2025-2026 (anecdotal, not official stats).

Why £2m Is The Standard For Handyman Work In The UK

Insurers settled on £2m public liability for general maintenance because it covers the realistic worst-case in a domestic setting. If you flood a flat in Edinburgh, damage a £45k kitchen, or a ladder falls onto a parked car, claims land between £3,000 and £80,000 in most cases. £2m gives headroom for legal costs and multiple claimants. Commercial clients often push for £5m because they have public footfall and contractual indemnity clauses that require higher limits. If you quote for a retail shop in Cardiff or a school in Oxford, check their contractor pack early. They will state the minimum.

You also need to think about territorial limits. Most Handyman Insurance UK policies cover England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland for work carried out in the UK only. If you do occasional jobs across the border in Ireland or online advice for overseas clients, tell your insurer. Some Simply Business and Hiscox wordings include temporary EU work up to 30 days, but many budget policies do not.

Document Retention and Claim Success

Insurers do not just pay because you say a tool was stolen. They ask for proof of ownership, forced entry, and police reference. Keep photos of serial numbers, receipts in Dropbox, and a short video walk-around of your van vault. For liability claims, keep signed job sheets. A simple line saying "Customer accepts that shelf location chosen by them, wall is plasterboard with no studs found using detector, weight limit advised 20kg" can save you.

FAQ - Handyman Insurance UK 2026

1. Do I legally need handyman insurance in the UK?

No single law says every handyman must have public liability, but employers' liability is legally required if you hire anyone, even part-time, under the Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969. Most commercial clients and councils require £2m-£5m public liability to give you work. Check HSE and FCA guidance.

2. How much public liability do I need as a handyman?

Most UK handymen hold £2m public liability. Domestic homeowners accept it, and it covers most slip, trip and property damage claims. If you work for letting agents, schools or commercial buildings, they often demand £5m. Start at £2m and upgrade only when contract requires.

3. Can I do plumbing or electrical jobs on handyman insurance?

Only minor, non-notifiable tasks. Like-for-like tap washer changes, replacing a socket faceplate. Full bathroom installs, gas work, or consumer unit rewires require specialist plumber insurance, electrician insurance and correct certification (Gas Safe, Part P). Standard handyman wording excludes them.

4. Does handyman insurance cover my tools if stolen from my van?

Yes, if you add tools cover and meet conditions. Insurers typically require forced entry evidence, van locked, and sometimes tools in a secured vault overnight. Cover is usually £1k-£10k. Overnight on-street storage may be excluded unless declared. Always keep receipts and photos.

5. What's the cheapest handyman insurance UK in 2026?

Illustrative ranges observed 08 May 2026 show basic £2m cover from £13.10-£32.90/month across AXA, Direct Line, Aviva, Hiscox and Simply Business. Cheapest is usually via a comparison broker like Simply Business with low turnover, £100-£250 excess, and no recent claims. Prices vary by location and activities.

6. Is handyman insurance tax deductible for self-employed?

Generally yes, as a legitimate business expense for self-employed in the UK. HMRC allows insurance costs wholly for trade. Keep certificate and payment proof. This is not tax advice - check gov.uk self-employed expenses guide or ask an accountant.
