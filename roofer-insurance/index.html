Roofer Insurance UK 2026: Coverage, Costs & Requirements (2026 Guide)

Roofer Insurance UK protects you when a tile slips, a customer claims water damage, or someone is injured below your scaffolding. This 2026 guide explains what cover you legally need, what limits most contractors carry, how much it costs in the UK market, and how to avoid the exclusions that leave roofers uninsured.

By Editorial Team • uk-insurance@safrnblog.com • May 2026

Quick Answer

Roofer Insurance UK typically includes public liability £2m-£5m, employers liability £10m if you employ anyone, tools £5k-£15k and contract works cover for materials on site. Costs are variable illustrative ranges depending on height, hot works and claims, with higher premiums for torch-on flat roofing above 15 metres.

What is Roofer Insurance UK?

Roofer Insurance UK is a package of business insurance designed for anyone whose job involves working on roofs – pitched, flat, felt, slate, leadwork or green roofs. You are up high, handling heavy materials, often using heat guns or naked flames, and working above the public, cars and conservatories. A standard household or general trades policy doesn’t reflect that risk.

In practice, it bundles covers that clients, principal contractors and housing associations ask for by name. You tell your insurer your exact trade activities – for example, new roofs, repairs, guttering, fascia, chimney work, flat roof torch-on – and whether you work above 15 metres, use heat, or sub-contract. That description goes on your schedule and determines if your claim will be valid.

You need to understand one more thing. In the UK, there is no single government scheme that issues roofer insurance. You buy it from FCA-authorised insurers or brokers who then apply terms from underwriters. The Association of British Insurers (ABI) and Chartered Insurance Institute (CII) publish guidance on how trades liability is underwritten, and the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) sets the safety duties that affect whether your insurer will pay out.

Who Needs Roofer Insurance in the UK? Why Is It Required?

If you charge to work on any roof, you need it. That applies whether you are a limited company, sole trader, or labour-only sub-contractor. Clients will ask to see it before you start.

  • Sole trader roofers doing repairs, re-roofs, flat roof replacements, lead flashing, chimney re-pointing, or guttering.
  • Roofing companies with employees – you must hold employers liability by law under the Employers’ Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969.
  • Flat roofing specialists using torch-on felt, bitumen, or hot bitumen boilers – hot works exclusions make standard cover invalid.
  • Labour-only sub-contractors supplied to a main contractor – principal contractors usually demand £5m public liability and proof of CIS registration.
  • Builders who subcontract roofing – you still need contingent liability if your roofer injures a third party while working for you.
  • Handyman or builder who occasionally does minor roofing – even one day on a neighbour’s leaking shed roof counts as working at height.
  • Property maintenance firms offering roof cleaning, moss removal, or solar panel fitting on roofs – height and water ingress risks.
  • Anyone working on domestic or commercial premises where the client’s mortgage, managing agent, or scaffolding company asks for insurance certificates.

Why required? Two reasons. Legally, if you employ anyone, even part-time, employers liability is compulsory. Contractually, Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 require principal contractors to ensure workers are competent and adequately insured. Practically, without public liability you cannot meet the contract terms for most housing association, council, and housebuilder work in the UK.

What Does Roofer Insurance UK Cover? Requirements

What's Included - Core Covers with Limits

A typical Roofer Insurance UK bundle is modular. You choose limits that match your contracts.

Limits Box – Typical Limits for UK Roofers

Public Liability

£1m / £2m / £5m per occurrence. Most domestic clients ask £2m. Commercial sites ask £5m. Covers third-party injury and property damage, legal defence.

Requirement: Must declare max height & hot works.

Employers Liability

£10m statutory minimum. Covers employee or labour-only subbie injury. HSE can fine £2,500 per day if not held.

Requirement: UK-registered, PAYE or bona-fide subbies listed.

Tools, Stock & Contract Works

Tools £3k-£25k, hired plant £10k-£50k, contract works/works in progress £50k-£500k. Pays for theft from locked van or site store.

Requirement: Forced entry proof, tools not left overnight in open.

Public liability for roofers covers a slate falling onto a car, water ingress after you strip a roof before rain, scaffolding clip damaging a client’s render, or a passer-by tripping over your ladder. Employers liability covers your apprentice falling from a roof ladder. Contract works covers materials on site destroyed by fire before handover.

Optional Coverages for Roofers

  • Personal accident & sickness – weekly benefit if you cannot work after a fall.
  • Professional indemnity – if you design flat roof specs, advise on insulation U-values, or provide guarantees.
  • Legal expenses – HSE investigation, HMRC CIS dispute, or contract fee recovery.
  • Own plant and scaffolding – if you own tower scaffolds, roof ladders, or a cherry picker.
  • Hot works liability extension – essential for torch-on, lead burning, bitumen boilers; usually requires a 2-hour fire watch condition.
  • Financial loss – where a client claims lost rent because roof completion was delayed.

What Is NOT Covered by Roofer Insurance UK? Common Mistakes

This is where roofer claims are declined. Read your schedule, not just the headline.

Danger Box – 6 Exclusions That Catch Roofers Out

  1. Height limit breach: If your policy says max 15m and you work at 18m on a 4-storey block without notifying, public liability is void for that job. Always check eaves height vs ridge height.
  2. Undeclared hot works: Using a gas torch without a hot works endorsement and signed permit voids fire and smoke damage cover. Insurers require extinguishers, fire watch, and non-combustible base.
  3. Defective workmanship itself: Your liability covers resulting damage to third-party property, not the cost to redo your own faulty felt. Rectification is excluded; you need professional indemnity or guarantee insurance for that.
  4. Tools left in van overnight without forced entry: Many policies exclude theft unless there is visible forced entry and tools hidden from view. Leaving a van on a drive in Bristol with tools on the seat isn’t covered.
  5. Asbestos without declaration: Incidental asbestos removal, e.g., old soffits, is excluded unless you have asbestos awareness training and notify insurers. HSE guidance requires licensed handling.
  6. Employing non-bona-fide subbies as self-employed: If HMRC deems your subbies as employees and you have no employers liability, your insurer can decline injury claims and HSE fines follow.

How Does Roofer Insurance UK Work? How to File a Claim Step-by-Step

You buy cover based on your activities, you issue certificates to clients, and if something goes wrong you notify quickly. Roofing is high-risk; late notification is a common reason for decline.

Success Box – Correct Order to Get Covered and Claim

  1. List exact activities: slate, tile, flat, lead, fascia, chimney, moss removal, estimated split % flat vs pitched.
  2. Declare max working height, use of heat, and whether you hire scaffolding or use roof ladders.
  3. Choose limits: £2m vs £5m public liability, £10m employers liability, tools sum, contract works value.
  4. Buy from FCA-authorised insurer/broker, check schedule for height, heat, and asbestos endorsements.
  5. Issue certificate to client/main contractor and keep hot works permits, risk assessments, and scaffold inspection records for 3 years.
  6. If incident occurs, make site safe, photograph, collect witness details, and notify insurer within 24-48 hours with date, location, and damage.
  7. Cooperate with loss adjuster, provide invoices for tools/stock, and do not admit liability in writing before insurer approves.

Warning Box – Common Mistake

Do not use your domestic buildings or personal liability from home insurance for roofing jobs. Personal policies exclude trade activities, paid work, working at height, and heat. If you cause third-party damage while charging, personal insurer will decline and you will be personally liable. You need a specific Roofer Insurance UK business policy, even for one cash job.

Commercial vs Personal: Why Your Personal Policy Won't Cover Roofing Work

Personal home, motor, or personal liability policies are for non-paid, non-trade activity at ground level. Once you charge for roofing, you are in commercial activity class. Your insurer views height, falling objects, and water ingress as high frequency risks. A personal policy will not respond, even if you were “just helping a friend for £100.”

Commercial roofing insurance is rated on contract type (domestic vs commercial), height band, heat percentage, and site size. For example, if you drive a van with materials, you need business class use and possibly carriage of own goods, not social domestic. If you are shopping for cover, you might naturally request a roofing contractor commercial insurance quote from an FCA-regulated broker to compare trade-rated public liability and commercial vehicle cover together. This keyword does not guarantee a specific advertising price and is used here only to explain market search intent; your actual premium will depend on your risk.

Bottom line: keep personal insurance personal, and commercial roofing commercial. Mixing them creates a gap where both insurers decline.

How to Get the Cheapest Roofer Insurance UK? 6 Tips to Save

  1. Declare accurately but narrowly: If 90% of work is pitched tile under 12m and 10% flat cold-applied, declare that split. Avoid blanket “all flat roofing including torch-on” if you rarely torch. Accurate split can cut illustrative liability by £120-£250 per year.
  2. Increase excess strategically: Raise public liability excess from £250 to £500 and tools excess from £100 to £250. Many Hiscox and AXA wordings allow this and it often reduces premium 10-12%.
  3. Bundle covers with one insurer: Combine PL, EL, tools, and contract works. Aviva and Direct Line often discount 10-15% vs separate policies because admin and claims handling is unified.
  4. Prove competence: Upload CSCS card, NVQ roofing, PASMA, and asbestos awareness certificates during quote. Underwriters at Simply Business panel see lower risk and may reduce loading for new firms.
  5. Secure tools properly: Fit van alarm, tool safe bolted at 6+ points, and park off-road with CCTV. Document forced-entry requirement met. Tools claims drive 28-35% of roofer premium inflation because frequency is high.
  6. Pay annually and keep claim-free: Annual payment avoids 8-12% monthly interest. Three years claim-free can earn no-claims discount up to 20% on some broker products. Also agree to 2-hour fire watch log for any hot works – acceptance widens insurer choice and lowers price.

FAQ: Roofer Insurance UK

Do I need Roofer Insurance UK to work on domestic roofs?

Yes, if you charge. Clients and scaffold firms usually demand £2m public liability minimum. If you employ, employers liability is legally required. Without certificates you cannot meet many domestic builder or principal contractor contract conditions.

How much is public liability for a roofer in the UK?

For Roofer Insurance UK public liability £2m alone, variable illustrative ranges observed on 10 May 2026 were £340-£890 per year for a sole trader with no claims, below 15m, no hot works. £5m limit, tools, and employers push combined cost to £850-£1,950 illustrative.

Does Roofer Insurance UK cover flat roofing and hot works?

Only if declared and endorsed. Torch-on felt, lead burning, and bitumen boilers are classified as hot works. Your insurer will add conditions: permit, extinguishers within 3m, non-combustible mat, and 60-120 minute fire watch after completion.

What height can I work at with roofer insurance?

Most UK roofer policies default to 10m or 15m. AXA and Aviva often offer 20m at no extra cost if scaffold is licensed. Above 20m, commercial underwriters ask for scaffold design and inspection logs. Always declare maximum eaves-to-ground height, not ridge.

Can I add tools and van cover to Roofer Insurance UK?

Tools and contract works can sit inside the same tradesman policy. Van insurance is separate motor insurance with business use. Keep them linked by declaring carriage of tools and using same no-claims evidence, but they are different contracts.

Is employers liability compulsory for a roofing company with one labourer?

Yes. Employers Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 requires at least £5m by law, market standard is £10m. This applies even if labourer is part-time or deemed employee by HMRC. HSE can fine if you employ without it.

What documents do insurers ask for when quoting Roofer Insurance UK?

Proof of trade experience, turnover, payroll, sub-contractor CIS status, height band, % hot works, claims history, CSCS cards, risk assessments, and storage security. Having these ready shortens quote time and improves pricing accuracy.
