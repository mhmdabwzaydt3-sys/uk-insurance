What is Professional Indemnity Insurance?

Professional indemnity insurance – often called PI – is a commercial liability policy that pays defence costs and damages if a client alleges your professional work was negligent, inaccurate, or incomplete and caused them financial loss. Unlike public liability, it is not about a slip or physical damage. It is about the advice you gave. Or the design you delivered. Or the deadline you missed.

You must understand this distinction. Why? Because a standard business contents or public liability policy will not respond to a claim for financial loss arising from bad advice. You need a specific PI wording.

Short answer: If you charge for expertise, you need cover for that expertise failing.

Who Needs It? Why Is It Required?

Not every business has the same exposure. But if your work is intangible – advice, code, design, strategy, compliance – the risk is very real. One line of wrong code can halt a client's e-commerce site. One miscalculated beam can delay a build.

You are more likely to need Best Professional Indemnity Insurance UK if you fall into one of these categories. This is not exhaustive. Check your professional body.

Checklist: Who Typically Needs Professional Indemnity Insurance in the UK
  • ✓ IT consultants, developers, SaaS agencies – code errors, missed deadlines, IP claims
  • ✓ Management, marketing and business consultants – negligent advice causing client loss
  • ✓ Architects, engineers, surveyors – design errors, breach of building regulations (RIBA/ARB often mandate PI)
  • ✓ Accountants, bookkeepers, financial advisers – FCA-regulated, PI often compulsory via professional rules
  • ✓ Designers, photographers, copywriters, recruiters – IP infringement, defamation, error in deliverables
  • ✓ Life coaches, trainers, therapists (non-medical) – alleged breach of duty or confidentiality
  • ✓ Contractors working via personal service company – most client contracts require £1m PI minimum
  • ✓ Any business holding client data or handling regulated advice – GDPR-related claims and regulatory investigation costs where covered

Is it a legal requirement? Under UK law there is no general statute requiring PI for all businesses. The Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 requires employers' liability – that's different. For PI, the requirement comes from regulators. For example, solicitors regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority must hold PI that meets Minimum Terms. Architects Registered Board requires PI. Many client Master Service Agreements demand £1m or £2m. You must read your contract. If your client asks for proof, you pay.

Optional Coverages

You can often bolt on other covers when buying professional indemnity, especially via brokers like Simply Business or Direct Line. You might need them. You might not.

  • Public liability: £1m to £5m for slip/trip or property damage when visiting clients. Often bundled for £30-£80 extra per year (illustrative, VARIABLE).
  • Cyber and data liability: For GDPR breach, data loss, ransomware response. Important for IT consultants. Limits £25k to £250k.
  • Directors and officers (D&O): For Ltd company directors facing personal claims.
  • Legal expenses: Tax investigations, employment disputes, contract disputes – not PI but often added.
  • Business interruption – professional: Some PI policies include interruption if you cannot practice due to investigation.

Not all insurers cover all optional covers. Hiscox tends to offer stronger cyber and technology E&O extensions. AXA focuses on bundled SME packs. Aviva offers flexible add-ons via broker channels. You must compare wordings, not just price.

What is NOT Covered / Strictly Excluded / Common Mistakes

This is where people get caught. You pay for a policy, assume you are covered, then find your claim is excluded. Read this carefully.

⛔ Danger – Strict Exclusions You Must Know
  1. Known claims or circumstances before inception: If you knew of a problem before buying cover and didn't disclose it, it is excluded. This is a claims-made trap. Retroactive date matters.
  2. Deliberate dishonesty, fraud, or intentional wrongdoing: PI never covers intentional misleading advice. Some policies exclude entire claim if any dishonesty proven.
  3. Bodily injury and property damage (unless extension): That's public liability territory. PI focuses on financial loss. A builder's scaffold collapse injuring public is not PI – it's EL/PL.
  4. Employment disputes, fines, and trading debts: Failure to pay suppliers, HMRC fines, contractual penalties for late delivery unless specifically covered as mitigation costs.
  5. Patent infringement in US/Canada and high-risk territories: Many UK wordings restrict IP cover territorially. Check if you advise US clients.
  6. Insolvency or financial guarantees: PI does not guarantee your work will make money or cover you if you go insolvent.

How Does It Work? Step-by-Step

Professional indemnity works on a claims-made basis in the UK. That is critical. It means the policy that responds is the one in force when the claim is first made against you, not when the work was done. So you need continuous cover and careful run-off cover when you stop trading.

✅ How a PI Claim Works – 6 Steps
  1. Client alleges negligence: You receive email/letter claiming your design or advice caused financial loss. Do not admit liability.
  2. Notify insurer immediately: Most UK policies require notification as soon as practicable. Late notification can void cover. Call broker/insurer and submit claim form.
  3. Insurer appoints legal team: FCA-regulated insurers must treat customers fairly. They will appoint solicitors – e.g., Hiscox uses specialist PI panels – and assess if negligence likely.
  4. Defence and negotiation: Legal team defends, negotiates, or settles. Defence costs may be inside your limit (eroding limit) or in addition – check wording.
  5. Settlement or court award: If liable, insurer pays compensation up to limit minus excess (typical excess £250-£1,000 for small firms, VARIABLE).
  6. Run-off consideration: If you retire, you need run-off cover (often 6 years in UK due to Limitation Act 1980) to stay protected for past work.

⚠️ Warning – Common Mistake

Many freelancers let PI lapse when between contracts. Because PI is claims-made, a gap means later claim for old work is not covered unless retroactive date is maintained. Hypothetical mistake: IT contractor in Leeds cancelled PI Jan 2026, claim made March 2026 for work done Nov 2025 – no live policy at claim date = no cover. You need continuous cover or run-off. Check retroactive date on schedule.

Factors Affecting Cost

Why does one designer pay £110 and another £890 for similar limits? Insurers assess risk. You must understand the levers so you can control them.

  • Profession and hazard: IT and construction-related consultancy is higher hazard than copywriting. Architects need higher limits due to RIBA/ARB requirements.
  • Turnover and contract size: £250k turnover vs £30k turnover – bigger client losses possible. Some insurers rate on fees, not turnover.
  • Limit of indemnity: £2m costs more than £250k. Roughly 40-80% higher going from £1m to £2m (illustrative VARIABLE).
  • Claims history and retroactive date: Prior claims may increase premium 25-100%. Longer retroactive cover may increase premium but reduces gaps.
  • Employees, subcontractors, worldwide cover: Staff doing professional work raises exposure. US/Canada cover drives price up significantly – not all insurers cover it.
  • Excess and aggregate vs any-one-claim: Higher voluntary excess (£1,000 vs £250) can reduce premium ~10-15% (illustrative). Aggregate limits are cheaper than any-one-claim but offer less total protection if multiple claims.

Cost varies by postcode? Slightly. London consultants may pay more due to higher claim values. Your qualification matters too – chartered status (e.g., CII for insurance professionals, CIPD for HR) may reduce premium with some insurers because it signals competence.

Commercial vs Personal / Business vs Domestic

Here is where people confuse personal and commercial cover. Personal professional policies – like personal liability on home insurance – do not cover you for paid client work. You are in business. You need commercial cover.

Think of it this way. If you advise a friend for free, that is personal. If you invoice for advice, even £50, that is business. PI must be commercial. Similarly, driving to a client meeting in your own car on a standard domestic motor policy may not cover business use. That is why you might need separate business vehicle insurance or a commercial auto insurance quote check to ensure Class 1 business use is included. Insurers view business mileage as higher risk. You must declare it.

For professional indemnity, commercial classification means: cover is for your trade, assessed on turnover, includes legal persons (Ltd company) as insured, and excludes private life. Not all insurers cover both personal and commercial – Aviva and AXA keep them separate, while Direct Line offers SME commercial packs that bundle PI, public liability and employers' liability. Check your policy's business description matches Companies House reality. If you do residential and commercial work, ensure wording does not restrict to domestic clients only.

How to Get the Cheapest Professional Indemnity Insurance

You want best cover without overpaying. You can. But you must be strategic, not just buy cheapest limit on comparison site. Here are 6 practical tips with real numbers.

  1. Match limit to contract, not ego: If your biggest client requires £1m, buy £1m – not £5m. HYPOTHETICAL: £250k to £1m upgrade often adds £90-£200 per year for low-risk professions; £1m to £5m may add £400+ (VARIABLE). Don't over-insure.
  2. Bundle PI + public liability correctly: Buying both from same insurer often saves 15-25% vs separate policies. Illustrative: Simply Business bundle £250k PI £1m PL observed £132/year vs separate £95 + £85 = £180. Saving ~£48. Check bundling discount.
  3. Increase voluntary excess to £500-£1,000: If you can afford excess, ask broker to quote £500 vs £250. Many SMEs see 10-12% reduction. If annual premium £350, new premium ~£308-£315 – saving £35-£42 per year.
  4. Maintain continuous cover and clean retroactive date: Don't let policy lapse between contracts. A lapse causes loss of retroactive cover. Keeping retroactive date from first policy inception can avoid higher new-business loading. Some insurers charge 20% extra for full retroactive cover after gap.
  5. Reduce turnover declaration accuracy & limit subcontractors: Report actual UK turnover, not global projected if you only trade UK. If you use subcontractors, ensure they hold their own PI with £1m limit – some insurers reduce your premium if you evidence this. Can save £80-£150 (illustrative).
  6. Use broker panel and compare 3 quotes annually: Don't auto-renew. Hiscox may be cheapest one year, AXA next. Comparison via Simply Business, Direct Line, Aviva broker quotes plus professional body scheme (e.g., CII member scheme) often finds 12-18% variation for same risk. Set renewal reminder 21 days before expiry – UK insurers must provide renewal notice per FCA rules.

FAQ

How much is professional indemnity insurance per month in the UK?

For low-risk freelancers, illustrative monthly ranges observed are £7 to £35 per month for £100k to £250k limits in August 2026, depending on profession and turnover. Higher-risk consultants or firms with £1m+ turnover often pay £50 to £150+ per month. Always get a live quote as prices vary significantly.

Do I legally need professional indemnity insurance in the UK?

There is no general UK statute making PI compulsory for all businesses, unlike Employers' Liability under the 1969 Act. However, it is required by some regulators like the Solicitors Regulation Authority and RIBA, and often contractually required by clients. Check your professional body rules and client contracts.

What is the best level of professional indemnity cover for consultants?

Most UK consultants start with £250,000 to £1 million. IT contractors and management consultants handling larger financial decisions often choose £1m to £2m because client contracts demand it. Regulated professions may need higher. The limit should reflect your contract value and worst-case claim.

Does professional indemnity insurance cover public liability?

No. Professional indemnity covers financial loss from negligent advice, errors, or omissions. Public liability covers bodily injury or property damage to third parties. You need both if you meet clients in person or visit sites. Many insurers bundle them for freelancers at a lower combined cost.

Is professional indemnity insurance tax deductible in the UK?

Yes, HMRC generally allows professional indemnity premiums as an allowable business expense if the cover is wholly for business purposes, reducing your taxable profit. Keep your policy schedule and proof of payment. Check GOV.UK self-employment expenses guidance or ask your accountant for your circumstances.

How do I choose the best professional indemnity insurer in the UK?

Compare FCA-regulated insurers like Hiscox, AXA, Aviva and specialist brokers such as Simply Business. Look at retroactive date, legal defence costs cover, excess level, and whether cover is on an any-one-claim or aggregate basis. Read policy wording and check ABI member status.
