Painter and Decorator Insurance UK [2026 Guide]: Coverage, Costs & Requirements

Painter and Decorator Insurance UK protects self-employed decorators and painting contractors from costly claims for property damage, injury and faulty work allegations. This 2026 guide breaks down what genuine trade cover must include, the average cost of painter and decorator insurance in the UK right now, and practical requirements to work on residential and commercial sites without risking your business.

Quick Answer — Featured Snippet

Painter and Decorator Insurance UK typically bundles public liability (£1m-£5m), employers’ liability if you hire staff, and tools cover up to £5,000. Most sole traders pay from £6-£14/month for basic liability, while comprehensive trade packs with professional indemnity cost £18-£38/month in the UK in 2026.

What is Painter and Decorator Insurance?

Imagine turning up to repaint a Victorian terrace in Bristol, drop cloths down, then a tin of trade white slips from a first-floor sill onto a client's stone patio set. That's exactly why Painter and Decorator Insurance UK exists. It is not a single policy, but a tradesman bundle assembled for the specific risks of painting and decorating work — splashes, solvent damage, ladder falls, colour mismatches, and theft of tools from a van overnight.

You will see similar bundles sold as decorating liability cover, painting trade insurance, or decorator business insurance. The core idea is the same: combine liability protections that landlords, letting agents and main contractors will demand proof of before you step on site. Unlike a generic personal liability add-on from a home insurer, a trade-specific policy recognises you work in other people's properties for reward, use heat guns, work at height, and handle client property daily.

The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) regulates how insurers market these trade products in the UK, while the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) sets the safety duties you must still meet even when insured. Having cover does not replace risk assessments, but it does mean a single accident does not wipe out months of income.

Who Needs Painter and Decorator Insurance / Why Is It Required?

Whether you call yourself a house painter, wallpapering specialist, spray finisher or multi-trade decorator, once money changes hands for work in another person's property, you step outside domestic cover. I see this mistake daily among new trades — assuming a home insurance liability extension will protect a paid job.

Checklist: You likely need cover if you:

  • Are self-employed, sole trader or limited company director doing painting/decorating for reward
  • Work in residential homes, rental flats, shops, offices or new builds
  • Use ladders, trestles, scaffolding or powered access under 10 metres (or higher if declared)
  • Hire labour-only subcontractors, apprentices or part-time help — triggers employers’ liability
  • Store paints, tools, spray equipment or wallpaper worth over £500 in a van or lockup
  • Are asked for a certificate of insurance by a letting agent, council, builder or main contractor
  • Give colour consultancy, design advice or specification — potential professional indemnity exposure
  • Work away from a fixed premises and need insurance to meet CITB site rules or HSE expectations

Even if not legally compulsory for sole traders, many clients will not let you start without £2m public liability. For public sector or commercial contracts, £5m is often the minimum in the tender. The Association of British Insurers (ABI) notes that trade liability claims for property damage average in the low thousands, but personal injury claims can run to tens of thousands quickly.

What Does Painter and Decorator Insurance Cover? Requirements Explained

What's Included — with Limits Box

Core painting trade insurance revolves around three pillars. Public liability covers injury to a third party or accidental damage to third-party property arising from your work. Employers’ liability covers injury to your employees. Professional indemnity, optional for decorators, covers financial loss arising from your professional advice, such as specifying the wrong fire-rated coating.

Limit: £1m / £2m / £5m
Typical excess: £100-£250

Tools & Business Equipment

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) does not require insurance to register as self-employed, but the Financial Conduct Authority expects you to hold adequate cover to trade responsibly if you employ people. Check your insurer wording for height limits — many standard decorator policies stop at 10 metres. If you spray exterior render from a cherry picker at 12 metres, you need that declared.

Optional Coverages to Consider

  • Contract Works / Own Plant: Covers materials and work-in-progress damaged by fire or vandalism before handover — useful for full-house repaints.
  • Professional Indemnity (PI): £50k-£500k limit. Important if you advise on paint systems, anti-damp solutions or heritage colour matching.
  • Personal Accident & Sickness: Weekly benefit if you cannot work after a fall — typically £150-£300/week for up to 52 weeks.
  • Legal Expenses: Up to £100k for contract disputes or HMRC investigations.
  • Stock and Materials in Transit: Paint and wallpaper in your van, not just tools.
  • Public Liability Extension for Heat Work: If you use heat guns for stripping — often excluded unless added and subject to hot-work conditions.

What is NOT Covered / Strictly Excluded / Common Mistakes

Every year decorators lose claims because they assumed cover was broader than it is. Below are gaps competitors often miss in generic tradesman guides.

⛔ DANGER — 6 Common Exclusions That Void Claims

  1. Faulty workmanship itself: Insurers pay for resultant damage, not for redoing your bad paint job. If the emulsion peels because you didn't prep, rectification is on you.
  2. Damage to property being worked on (if not extended): Standard PL may exclude the specific wall you are painting. You need a “work on” extension or contract works add-on.
  3. Unattended heat guns / blowtorches: Any hot-work claim without a completed hot-work permit and fire watch will be declined.
  4. Tools left in unlocked vehicle overnight: Overnight van theft requires forced entry evidence and often a Thatcham-approved lock. Soft tonneau covers rarely qualify.
  5. Working above declared height: If your policy says 10m and you’re on a scaffold at 13m in Leeds city centre, public liability may not respond.
  6. Asbestos disturbance or pollution: Scraping textured coatings that contain asbestos without an HSE-licensed plan is excluded and can lead to enforcement.

How Does It Work? / How to File a Claim Step-by-Step

Say a homeowner in Bath reports that masking tape pulled plaster off a freshly boarded ceiling. How do you move from complaint to claim without making it worse? The process is standard across FCA-regulated insurers like AXA, Aviva and Hiscox.

✅ SUCCESS PATH — 6 Steps to a Valid Claim

  1. Stop and mitigate: Make safe, take 20+ photos, keep damaged materials. Do not admit liability in writing.
  2. Notify insurer within 24-48 hours: Most policies require prompt notification. Use the claims line and policy number.
  3. Gather evidence pack: Contract, invoice, RAMS/ risk assessment, photos before/after, witness details.
  4. Log third-party contact: Forward any Letter of Claim to insurer unanswered; do NOT offer to pay cash.
  5. Allow loss adjuster/inspection: For claims over £1,500, expect a call within 3-5 working days.
  6. Settlement & excess: If liability accepted, insurer pays claimant minus your excess (£100-£250). Keep proof for HMRC expenses.

⚠️ WARNING — Common Mistake

Do not settle a client property damage claim in cash before telling your insurer. Many trade policies state that any admission or payment without insurer consent can void cover. Even a £200 goodwill gesture can be treated as an admission of liability. Always notify first, even if you intend to pay.

Factors Increasing or Decreasing Your Cost

  • Turnover: £30k turnover usually cheaper than £120k — higher turnover = larger jobs = bigger potential claims.
  • Claims in last 3-5 years: One property damage claim £2k paid can lift premium 20-35% at renewal.
  • Height and heat: Working above 10m or using heat guns adds £40-£120/year.
  • Number of employees: Each additional employee adds £80-£180/year for EL, per HSE exposure.
  • Excess level: Choosing £500 excess instead of £100 can reduce monthly cost by 10-15%, but means you pay more at claim time.
  • Tools limit and storage: Higher tools sum insured and overnight van storage increases premium.
  • Payment method: Annual payment avoids credit charges vs monthly.

Commercial vs Personal / Basic vs Pro — Why Trade Cover Matters

A personal liability extension or a free home insurance add-on does not cover you for reward. Once you invoice a client, UK insurers classify you as commercial. If you also drive a van full of ladders and paints, you should review whether you need a commercial van insurance quote alongside your trade liability — personal car insurance will usually exclude carriage of business equipment and materials for hire. This integration is why main contractors ask for both vehicle and liability proof; they are separate but linked protections, not one policy covering both.

Basic pro cover (£1m PL, no tools) might suit a side-hustle decorator painting one flat a month. Pro level (£2m-£5m PL + £10m EL if you have staff + tools £3k + contract works £10k) is what letting agencies, local authorities and house builders in the UK typically mandate under their supplier rules. The Chartered Insurance Institute (CII) recommends matching cover limits to contract values and worst-case loss scenarios, not just the cheapest option.

How to Get Cheapest Painter and Decorator Insurance — 6 Practical Tips

Keeping the average cost of painter and decorator insurance low is about evidence, not just shopping around. Insurers reward decorators who can show reduced risk.

  1. Declare accurate turnover tiers. Don’t round £42k up to £50k. Many insurers have bands at £25k, £50k, £75k — staying just inside a band (e.g., £48k) can save £60-£110/year versus crossing it.
  2. Increase voluntary excess carefully. Moving from £100 to £250 excess can cut basic premium by £4-£7/month (£48-£84/year) — only if you have cash to cover a claim.
  3. Bundle PL + tools + EL with one insurer. Single trade pack from AXA or Aviva is often 15-20% cheaper than three standalone policies. Simply Business comparison often shows bundle savings of £70-£150/year.
  4. Prove secure storage. Install a Thatcham-approved van lock (£120-£250 fitted) and provide photos. Some insurers discount tools cover by 10% and reduce excess from £250 to £100.
  5. Limit heat and height where possible. If you no longer use heat stripping, tell insurer to remove hot-work endorsement — can save £45-£90/year. Similarly, confirm max height 8m vs 15m.
  6. Pay annually and keep HSE training records. Annual payment avoids 9% APR credit charge (£18 on a £200 policy). A CITB Health & Safety Awareness certificate or PASMA ticket sometimes earns 5-7% discount with Hiscox and Direct Line commercial.

FAQ — Painter and Decorator Insurance UK

Is painter and decorator insurance legally required in the UK?

Public liability is not a statutory requirement for sole traders, but employers’ liability at £10m is legally required if you employ staff, including apprentices or labour-only subcontractors under your control. Many clients and sites make £2m-£5m public liability a contractual requirement.

How much public liability do painters and decorators need?

Most UK domestic clients ask for £2m. Letting agents, housing associations and commercial builders often require £5m. Insurers usually offer £1m, £2m and £5m. Consider your largest job value and worst-case damage — a paint spill on a £40k kitchen costs more than a £1m limit suggests, but legal costs push claims up.

Does Painter and Decorator Insurance UK cover damage to the property I’m working on?

Standard public liability often excludes the specific part you are actively working on. For example, damage to the wall you are painting may be excluded. Look for a “damage to property being worked upon” extension or add contract works cover. Always read the ABI-aligned wording.

Can I get painter and decorator insurance for one day or short term?

Some brokers via Simply Business or Direct Line offer short-term or single-day trade liability from around £20-£45 per day (VARIABLE, observed 02-May-2026). For regular work, annual is cheaper — often 4-5 days of daily cover equals a full year at £72-£168.

What is the average cost of painter and decorator insurance for a sole trader?

For a sole trader with no employees, no claims in 3 years and £2m public liability, illustrative range observed is £6-£14/month if paid annually. With tools £3k and legal expenses, range rises to £18-£28/month. With employees, add £10m employers’ liability — total illustrative £27-£42/month in 2026. Not guaranteed.

Does it cover tools stolen from my van overnight?

Tools cover usually applies if the van was locked, parked at your home or a secure location, and forced entry is evidenced. Unattended in a public car park overnight may breach conditions. Check your policy for “overnight unattended vehicle” clause and keep receipts and photos of tools to evidence £1k-£10k claims.
